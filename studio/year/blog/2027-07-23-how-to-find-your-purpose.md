"How do I find my purpose? Everyone else seems to know theirs."

It's a question that can sit in the back of your mind for years. Maybe your career is fine. Good, even. People would call you successful. And yet you have the nagging sense that you're supposed to be doing something more meaningful, and you have no idea what it is.

So you search. You take the personality tests. You read about finding your "why." You wait for the lightning bolt.

Here's a gentler truth: you probably don't need one grand purpose. And waiting for one might be what's keeping you stuck.

## Do I need a life purpose?

You need meaning. Most of us do. But meaning and a single, capital-P Purpose aren't the same thing.

The idea that everyone has one big calling, that you're supposed to find it and then everything clicks, puts enormous pressure on one question. If you don't have an answer, you feel behind. If you do have an answer and it changes, you feel like you got it wrong.

For a lot of people, purpose isn't a destination. It's more like a direction. It shows up in small moments of "yes, this," spread across work, relationships, creativity and care. You find it by noticing, not by deciding.

## Why can't I find my purpose?

A few common reasons:

- **You're too busy to notice.** Purpose is easy to miss when every hour is spoken for. It often shows up in White Space, the unscheduled time you rarely get.
- **You're looking for something impressive.** Your Ego wants a purpose that looks good on LinkedIn. Your Soul may want something quieter.
- **You're exhausted.** It's hard to feel called to anything when your nervous system is running on fumes.
- **You're afraid of what you'll find.** Sometimes we already have an inkling, and it would mean change.

If any of those sound familiar, you're not purposeless. You're just overloaded or searching in the wrong place.

## Why does knowing your purpose matter more now?

Work is changing fast. AI is reshaping a lot of jobs, and plenty of people aren't sure what their role will look like in a few years. Nobody knows exactly how it will shake out. But if your whole identity rests on a job title, or on the 9-to-5 your degree put you in debt for, that's shaky ground to stand on.

Your gifts are steadier ground. The way you see things, the problems you're drawn to, the way people feel around you: those go with you to whatever comes next. You're here, now, for a reason. Knowing who you are is worth more than any one job description.

## How do I find my purpose, then?

Stop hunting for the big answer and start collecting small clues.

**1. Notice what makes time disappear.**
When do you look up and realize two hours have gone by? What were you doing?

**2. Notice what you can't stop caring about.**
What problems do you keep noticing, at work or in the world, that other people seem to walk past?

**3. Notice who you help without being asked.**
What do friends and coworkers come to you for? That's often a clue to your gifts.

**4. Notice what drains you, too.**
Purpose is partly found by subtraction. What feels like wearing someone else's clothes?

**5. Say yes to small experiments.**
You don't need to quit your job. Volunteer for one project. Take one class. Have one conversation with someone doing work you're curious about. Purpose gets clearer through action, not more thinking.

## Try this: the 30-day YES! list

For the next 30 days, keep a note on your phone. Every time you feel a small "yes, this" in your body, a lift or a spark or a sense of rightness, write down what you were doing. Don't judge it. Don't make it mean anything yet.

At the end of the month, read it back. Look for patterns. That's not the whole picture of your purpose. But it's real data, and it came from your life, not a quiz.
