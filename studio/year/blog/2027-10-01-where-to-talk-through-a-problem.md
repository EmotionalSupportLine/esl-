"I just need to talk through a problem with someone."

Not get a lecture. Not get told what to do. Just say it out loud, think it through with another brain, and come out the other side knowing what you're going to do next.

That's one of the most useful things a human can do for another human. Here's where to find it.

## Why does talking through a problem help so much?

Because problems look different out loud.

In your head, a problem tends to loop. Thoughts repeat. Worries stack on top of each other. Everything feels urgent and tangled. When you say it to someone, you have to put it in order. First this, then this, then this. Just doing that often shows you the answer, or at least the next step.

There's also your body. When you're stressed, your nervous system is on alert, and it's hard to think clearly. Being with a calm person helps your body settle, and a settled body thinks better. That's called co-regulation. Read more about it in [what co-regulation is](/blog/what-is-co-regulation).

## Who can I talk through a problem with?

Different people are good for different problems. Here's a quick guide.

- **A friend** is great for everyday problems, especially when they know the people involved. Less great when they have a strong opinion or a stake in what you decide.
- **A mentor or someone a few steps ahead** is great for career, business or life-stage questions.
- **A coach** is great when the problem is a goal and you need a plan and accountability over time.
- **A financial professional** is the right call for money decisions, investing or debt plans.
- **A lawyer** is the right call for legal problems.
- **Your doctor or a licensed therapist** is the right call if the problem involves your health, ongoing depression or anxiety, trauma, or anything that needs diagnosis or treatment.
- **A trained emotional supporter** is great when you need a calm, neutral person to help you sort out your feelings and find a next step.

That last one is what we do.

## What if the problem involves the people I'd normally talk to?

This happens all the time. The problem is your partner, so you can't talk to your partner. The problem is your sister, so you can't talk to your mom. The problem is at work, so you can't talk to anyone at work.

That's when a neutral person is worth a lot. Someone outside your life doesn't have a side to take. They're not going to tell anyone. They don't need you to choose one way or the other. They can just help you think.

## How do I get the most out of talking a problem through?

Try this simple structure. It works with a friend, a supporter or even a notebook.

1. **Name the problem in one sentence.** "I don't know whether to take the new job."
2. **Say how you feel about it.** Excited? Scared? Guilty? All three? Feelings are information.
3. **List what you already know.** Facts, not fears. Deadlines, money, what matters to you.
4. **Brainstorm without judging.** Every option on the table, even the silly ones.
5. **Pick one next step.** Not the whole answer. Just the next real action.

If you only have five minutes, skip to steps 1 and 5.
