"What am I doing with my life?"

Maybe it hit you on a Sunday night, dreading Monday. Maybe in the car after another long day. Maybe scrolling past an old friend's announcement: new business, new city, new everything. And you sat there thinking, *wait. How did I get here? Is this it?*

It's an unsettling question. It can make your stomach drop. But it's not a sign that something is wrong with you. More often, it's a sign that something in you is waking up.

## Why do I keep asking "what am I doing with my life?"

The question tends to show up at certain moments:

- **After a milestone.** A birthday ending in zero, a promotion, the kids leaving, a parent getting older.
- **When the old plan is done.** You hit the goals you set at 25 and they don't feel the way you expected.
- **When you're running on empty.** Exhaustion has a way of making everything feel pointless.
- **When something you value has been missing for a while.** Creativity, adventure, connection, rest.
- **When your life looks right on paper but doesn't feel like yours.**

None of those mean you've wasted your life. They mean you're ready to check in with it.

## Is it normal to feel lost in your career or life?

Yes. It's one of the most common human experiences there is. People feel it at 24, at 37, at 52, at 68. It doesn't mean you made the wrong choices. Often it means the choices that were right for a past version of you aren't quite right for who you are now.

The tricky part is that most of us feel it alone. We assume everyone else has it figured out. They don't.

## Why are so many people asking this right now?

Part of it is work. AI is reshaping a lot of jobs quickly, and many people feel unsure about where their role is headed. No one can say exactly what happens next. But when your sense of who you are is built on a job title, uncertainty about the job turns into uncertainty about you.

That's why this question deserves a real answer, not a panicked one. Who you are is bigger than your corporate gig or the career your degree pointed you toward. Your gifts, your values and the way you show up for people come with you, whatever work looks like. You're here, now, for a reason. Knowing yourself is the steadiest ground there is.

## What should I do when I feel this way?

First, don't blow anything up. The urge to quit the job, sell the house or move across the country can be strong. Sometimes it's right. But big moves made from panic usually just move the panic somewhere else.

Instead, try this order.

**1. Settle.**
You can't think clearly about your whole life while your body is in alarm mode. Take a walk, breathe slowly, try a few long, slow exhales with your feet on the floor. Come back to the question when you're calmer.

**2. Get curious, not critical.**
Swap "what am I doing with my life?" (which usually sounds like an accusation) for "what is my life asking for right now?" (which sounds like a question).

**3. Look at what's actually missing.**
Not "everything." Something specific. Let the questions below help.

**4. Take one small step toward it.**
Not the whole move. One step.

## Try this: the life check-in

Rate each area from 1 to 10 for how it feels right now, not how it looks:

- Work
- Money
- Health and energy
- Relationships and friendship
- Time that's truly yours
- Creativity or play
- Meaning or contribution

Then look at your lowest one or two. Ask:

1. What would a 7 look like here? (Not a 10. A 7.)
2. What's one thing I could do this month to move it up by one point?

That's it. You don't have to solve your whole life. You just have to find the thread and pull it a little.
