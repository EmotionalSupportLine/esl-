"Grounding" has two meanings in our world. One is earthing: spending time barefoot on the earth. The other is a set of simple techniques for bringing your attention back to the present moment when your mind is racing.

This post is about the second kind, though you'll see they overlap. Every one of these takes five minutes or less, and you can do most of them anywhere.

## Why grounding helps

When you're anxious or overwhelmed, your mind tends to race ahead (worrying about the future) or back (replaying the past). Grounding techniques gently pull your attention into right now, through your senses and body. Many people find that helps them feel steadier and think more clearly.

## 1. The 5-4-3-2-1 senses check

Name, out loud or in your head:
- **5** things you can see
- **4** things you can touch
- **3** things you can hear
- **2** things you can smell
- **1** thing you can taste

It works because it's hard to spiral when you're busy noticing.

## 2. Feet on the floor

Press your feet into the ground. Notice the floor holding you. Wiggle your toes. If you can step outside onto grass or soil barefoot, even better.

## 3. Long exhale breathing

Breathe in for four counts. Breathe out for six to eight counts. Repeat five times. A long out-breath is a simple way to signal to your body that you're okay.

## 4. Hold something cold or textured

A cool glass of water, a smooth stone, a soft blanket. Focus completely on how it feels in your hands.

## 5. Name where you are

Say it plainly: "I'm in my kitchen. It's Tuesday. It's 3 pm. I'm safe right now." Simple facts can be surprisingly settling.

## 6. Look far away

Look out a window at something in the distance: a tree, a cloud, the horizon. Let your eyes soften.

## 7. Move a little

Stretch your arms overhead. Roll your shoulders. Walk around the room or the block. Gentle movement can release some of the restless energy.

## Build a grounding habit

These techniques work best when you've practiced them on calm days, so they're familiar when you need them. Earthing is a lovely daily anchor: a few minutes barefoot outside every morning.
