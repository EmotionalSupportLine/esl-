On **Thursday, September 23, 2027**, we reach the autumn equinox in the Northern Hemisphere. Day and night are nearly equal again. From here, the nights grow longer until the winter solstice in December.

In astrology, it's also when the sun moves into Libra, the sign of balance.

## What the equinox is

Twice a year, the sun shines directly over the equator, giving nearly equal hours of day and night across the planet. In spring, that balance tips toward light. In autumn, it tips toward dark.

Many cultures have marked the autumn equinox as a time of harvest, gratitude and preparation for winter.

## Why autumn is a season for letting go

The trees model it beautifully. They don't hang onto every leaf. They let go of what they don't need, so they can rest and grow again in spring.

The equinox is a natural moment to ask what you're ready to release.

## An equinox reflection

Find a quiet 20 minutes. If you can, sit outside as the sun sets.

**1. Balance check.** How balanced does your life feel between giving and receiving? Doing and resting? Others and yourself?

**2. What's ready to fall away?** A commitment, a role, a habit, a worry, a version of yourself you've outgrown. Write it down.

**3. What do I want to protect through the darker months?** Rest, time outside, a creative practice, a friendship.

**4. What needs tending before winter?** A conversation, a project, a home task, a habit.

If it feels right, take the paper with what you're releasing outside and recycle it, compost it or tear it up. Let it go with the leaves.

## Ways to mark the day

- Take a slow walk and notice the changing trees
- Watch the sunset
- Cook a seasonal meal with apples, squash or root vegetables
- Clear out one closet or drawer
- Write a letter to yourself to open at the spring equinox
