You might be doing okay most days. Then a date approaches, an anniversary, a birthday, a holiday, and the grief comes rushing back, sometimes as strongly as the beginning.

That's very common. Grief doesn't follow a calendar, but calendars can stir it up. Here's how to get through the hard dates.

## Why anniversaries hit so hard

Dates carry memory. Your body and mind often remember before you consciously realize the day is coming. Many people notice they feel heavier, more tired or more emotional in the days or weeks before an anniversary, without knowing why at first.

This is a normal part of grief. It doesn't mean you're going backward.

## Before the day

**Mark it on your calendar.** It sounds strange, but knowing it's coming helps you plan instead of being blindsided.

**Decide how you want to spend it.** Alone or with others? Busy or quiet? At home or somewhere meaningful?

**Tell someone.** Let a friend or family member know the date is hard for you. Ask them to check in.

**Lower expectations.** If possible, keep the day light: fewer obligations, gentler plans.

## Ways to spend the day

There's no right way. Some ideas:

- Visit a place connected to the person
- Cook their favorite meal
- Write them a letter
- Look through photos, or share stories with others who knew them
- Do something they loved
- Light a candle
- Spend time in nature
- Rest, and let yourself feel whatever comes

Some people prefer to keep busy. That's okay too.

## After the day

Grief can linger after an anniversary. Be gentle with yourself for a few days afterward.

## You don't have to carry it alone

Grief can feel isolating, especially as the years go by and others seem to have moved on. A support call gives you 30 minutes with a kind, trained person who will listen as you talk about who you've lost and what this time of year brings up.

If your grief has felt overwhelming for a long time, or it's affecting your ability to get through your days, a grief counselor or support group may also help.

*If grief ever leads to thoughts of suicide or of not wanting to be here, please call or text 988 (US) right away.*

Missing them is a measure of how much they mattered.
