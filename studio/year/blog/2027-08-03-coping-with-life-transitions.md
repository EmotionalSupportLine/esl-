A move. A new job, or the loss of one. A divorce. Retirement. A health scare in the family. A child leaving home. A loss.

Big life transitions shake the ground under your feet. Even good changes can leave you feeling unsteady, unsure who you are or what comes next.

## Why transitions are so hard

Transitions disrupt the things that usually keep us steady:

- **Routines** that structured our days
- **Roles** that told us who we were
- **People and places** we relied on
- **A sense of the future** we could picture

When those shift, it's normal to feel anxious, sad, restless or lost, even when the change was your choice.

## The in-between is real

Many people describe transitions in three stages: an **ending** (letting go of what was), a messy **in-between** (where the old is gone and the new isn't clear yet), and a **new beginning**.

The in-between is the hardest part. It's uncomfortable and uncertain. It's also where a lot of growth happens. You don't have to rush through it.

## Ways to steady yourself

**Keep a few anchors.** When everything is changing, small routines help: a morning walk, a regular call with a friend, the same bedtime. Pick two or three and protect them.

**Name what you've lost.** Even good changes involve loss. Let yourself grieve it.

**Lower your expectations.** You won't be at your most productive right now. That's okay.

**Take one decision at a time.** You don't need to have the whole new life figured out.

**Get outside.** Nature changes constantly and stays steady. Many people find time outdoors calming during big changes.

**Talk about it.** Transitions are lonely when you're carrying them silently.

## A steady voice in the middle of it

A support call gives you 30 minutes with a trained supporter to talk through what's changing, what you're feeling, and the one next step that would help most.

*If a life change ever leaves you feeling hopeless or thinking about suicide, please call or text 988 (US) right away.*
