On **Monday, June 21, 2027**, the Northern Hemisphere gets its longest day of the year. It's the summer solstice: we get the longest stretch of daylight and the sun climbs highest in the sky.

From here, the days slowly begin to shorten again. That makes the solstice a natural moment to pause at the peak of the light and take stock.

## What the solstice is

The solstice happens when the Earth's northern half is tilted most directly toward the sun. In astrology, it's also when the sun moves into Cancer.

People have marked this day for thousands of years with fires, festivals, gatherings at stone circles and simple time outdoors.

## Simple ways to mark the longest day

**Catch the sunrise.** Set an alarm, grab a blanket and watch the day begin. It's earlier than you'd think.

**Spend the whole day outside, or as much as you can.** Eat lunch outdoors. Take a walk at sunset. Let the day be about light.

**Go barefoot.** Warm grass, sand or soil underfoot is one of the simplest summer pleasures.

**Gather people.** A potluck in the backyard, a picnic in the park or a sunset walk with a friend.

**Watch the sunset.** Notice how late it is. Notice the long golden light.

## A solstice reflection

At the brightest point of the year, ask yourself:

1. **What is in full bloom in my life right now?**
2. **What have I grown since the winter solstice?**
3. **What do I want to enjoy fully this summer, before the days shorten?**
4. **What can I let ripen without rushing?**

Write your answers somewhere you'll find them again in December. It's a lovely thing to reread on the longest night.

## A note on sun safety

The longest day is also a strong UV day. Enjoy the morning and evening light freely. At midday, use shade, a hat and sunscreen.
