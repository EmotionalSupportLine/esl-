"Set boundaries" is advice everyone gives and almost nobody explains. It can sound like you need a carefully rehearsed speech, a confrontation and a dramatic exit.

You don't. Most good boundaries are one or two sentences, said calmly, and then repeated as needed.

## What a boundary actually is

A boundary isn't a rule for someone else. It's a decision about what *you* will do.

- "Don't call me after 9 pm" is a request.
- "I don't answer calls after 9 pm" is a boundary.

You can't control whether people respect a request. You can always control your boundary, because it's about your own actions.

## Signs you need one

- You feel resentful after spending time with someone
- You say yes and immediately wish you'd said no
- You dread certain calls, texts or visits
- You're exhausted from managing other people's feelings

Resentment is often a signal that a boundary is missing.

## Short, kind boundary scripts

**For time:**
"I can stay until 3, then I need to head out."

**For requests:**
"I can't take that on right now."
"That doesn't work for me, but I hope you find someone who can help."

**For topics:**
"I'm not going to talk about my weight. Tell me about your trip instead."

**For your energy:**
"I want to hear about this, but I don't have the capacity tonight. Can we talk Saturday?"

**For repeated requests:**
"I know you're disappointed. My answer is still no."

Notice what's missing: long explanations, apologies, justifications. You're allowed to keep it short. Over-explaining invites debate.

## When people push back

Some people won't like your boundary, especially if they benefited from you not having one. That's normal. Their discomfort isn't proof you did something wrong.

Try:
- **Repeat calmly.** Same words, same tone. "I hear you. It's still a no."
- **Follow through.** If you said you'd leave at 3, leave at 3.
- **Expect a wobble.** Relationships often adjust after a period of friction.

If a boundary is ever met with threats or harm, that's not a normal pushback, and your safety comes first. Call 911 if you're in danger.

## Start small

Practice with low-stakes boundaries first: declining a meeting, leaving an event early, not answering a text right away. Each one builds the muscle.
