On **Monday, December 21, 2026**, the Northern Hemisphere has its longest night of the year. It's the winter solstice.

After that night, the days start to lengthen again. Slowly at first, just seconds a day, then minutes, but steadily. For thousands of years, people have gathered around fires on this night to mark the return of the light.

You don't have to light a bonfire to feel the meaning of it.

## What the solstice is

The solstice is the moment the sun reaches its lowest point in the sky at noon, as seen from the Northern Hemisphere. Our side of the Earth is tilted furthest from the sun, which gives us the shortest day and the longest night. From here, the tilt slowly turns back.

In astrology, it's also the moment the sun moves into Capricorn.

## Why the darkest night can feel hopeful

Winter can be heavy. Short days, cold mornings, the pressure of the holidays. Many people feel lower in December.

The solstice offers a quiet, true reminder: the darkest point is also the turning point. Nothing dramatic happens. The light simply starts coming back, a little at a time. That's often how hard seasons in our own lives end too.

## A simple solstice reflection

Set aside 20 minutes on the evening of the 21st. Turn off the overhead lights and light a candle or a lamp. Then sit with three questions.

**1. What did this year ask of me?**
Let yourself list everything: the obvious challenges and the quiet ones nobody saw.

**2. What am I ready to leave in the dark?**
A habit, a worry, a role, a story about yourself that no longer fits. Write it down. If it feels right, tear the paper up.

**3. What do I want to grow as the light returns?**
Think of something small you'd like more of: rest, play, time outside, honesty, a creative project.

When you're done, blow out the candle. Notice that you're sitting in the dark, and that you're okay. Then turn the lights back on.

## Bring the light back in small ways

Over the next few weeks, look for small chances to welcome the light:

- Step outside in the morning, even for two minutes
- Open the curtains as soon as you wake up
- Take a walk at midday when the sun is highest
- Notice the sunset getting a little later each week
