"I need help with procrastination. I keep putting things off and I hate myself for it."

If that's you, first: put the self-hate down for a minute. It's not helping, and it's probably making the procrastinating worse.

Procrastination isn't a personality trait. It's a signal. Something about the task is jamming you up. Most procrastination advice treats every delay the same way: set a timer, break it into chunks, reward yourself. Those tips work sometimes. When they don't, it's because they're fixing the wrong problem.

There are three very different reasons people put things off. Find yours, and the fix gets a lot simpler.

## Why do I procrastinate even when I want to do the thing?

Because wanting to do something and being able to start it are two separate things. You can want to finish the quarterly report and still find yourself reorganizing your inbox. That gap between wanting and doing usually comes from one of three places.

**1. An information problem.**
You don't actually know the next step. The task on your list says "Q1 strategy" or "deal with the taxes," and your brain can't find a handle on it. So it slides off to something with a clearer edge, like email.

*How to tell:* if someone asked "what's the very first thing you'd do?" you couldn't answer right away.

**2. An emotion problem.**
You know exactly what to do, and doing it brings up a feeling you'd rather avoid. Fear of it not being good enough. Dread of a hard conversation. Shame about how long it's already taken. Your brain avoids the feeling by avoiding the task.

*How to tell:* the task is small and clear, but just thinking about it makes your stomach drop or your jaw clench.

**3. A capacity problem.**
You're out of fuel. Your days are packed, your sleep is short, and your nervous system has been in go-mode so long that every task looks like a mountain. This isn't procrastination as much as running on empty.

*How to tell:* it's not one task you're avoiding, it's almost everything. You're tired and wired at the same time.

We go deeper into the signs in [why can't I get anything done](/blog/why-cant-i-get-anything-done).

## What actually helps with procrastination?

Match the fix to the problem.

**For an information problem: shrink the step.**
Break the task down until the first step takes ten minutes or less and starts with a verb. Not "work on the proposal." "Open last year's proposal and copy the outline." If you can't name a first step, the first step is finding out: "Ask Sam what format they want."

**For an emotion problem: name the feeling.**
Say it plainly, ideally out loud. "I'm scared this will show I'm not as good as they think." "I'm dreading his reaction." Feelings lose a lot of their grip once they're named. Then do the smallest version of the task while the feeling is still there. You don't have to wait until you feel brave.

**For a capacity problem: settle before you start.**
Don't reach for a productivity hack. Reach for your nervous system. Take a few slow breaths with long exhales, step outside for five minutes, or try the [10-Minute Human Reset](/free/human-reset). Then cut something. Drop, delegate or do half as well one thing this week. You can't schedule your way out of having too much.

## A quick procrastination check

Next time you notice yourself avoiding something, try this:

1. Write the task at the top of a page.
2. Under it, write: "The first step is ___." If you can't fill it in, it's information.
3. Then write: "When I think about doing it, I feel ___." If a strong feeling shows up, it's emotion.
4. Then rate your energy from 1 to 10. If it's 3 or below, it's capacity.

It takes two minutes, and it stops you from beating yourself up with the wrong solution.

## Is procrastination ever a sign of something more?

Sometimes. If you're avoiding nearly everything, you've lost interest in things you used to enjoy, or you've felt low for weeks, that's worth talking to your doctor or a licensed therapist about. Not a crisis line. If you're in danger or thinking about suicide, call or text 988 (US).
