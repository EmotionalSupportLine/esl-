Five minutes of morning light. Ten minutes barefoot on the grass. One honest conversation a week. A pause at each new moon.

None of these look like much on a single day. That's exactly why they work.

## The quiet math of small habits

Small habits are easy to start and hard to see. Five minutes a day doesn't feel life-changing on Tuesday. But five minutes a day for a year is more than 30 hours. Thirty hours outside. Thirty hours of quiet. Thirty hours you gave yourself.

Small habits also change something less measurable: how you see yourself. Each time you show up, you're casting a small vote for the person you want to be.

## What can shift over a year

Everyone's different, and we can't promise any particular result. But people who keep small habits often notice things like:

- Feeling more connected to the seasons and the sky
- Having a few minutes each day that are truly theirs
- More trust in themselves, because they kept small promises
- A steadier baseline on hard days
- Noticing more: the light, the birds, the moon, their own needs

## Why small beats big

- **Small habits survive bad weeks.** Two minutes is doable even when life is chaotic.
- **They don't depend on motivation.** You don't need to feel inspired to step outside.
- **They grow naturally.** Five minutes often turns into fifteen when you're enjoying it.
- **They're easy to restart.** Missed a week? Just start again tomorrow.

## How to start (or start again)

1. **Pick one habit.** Morning light, earthing, a short walk, writing one sentence a day.
2. **Make it tiny.** Small enough that it's almost silly.
3. **Attach it to something you already do.** Coffee, school drop-off, lunch break.
4. **Track it.** A streak is motivating.
5. **Forgive gaps.** Missing a day is normal. Missing two is a signal to restart, not to quit.
