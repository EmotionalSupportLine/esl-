"What does a life coach do, exactly? Like, what happens when I get on the phone?"

This is the question nobody answers. Coaching websites are full of words like "transformation" and "unlock your potential," and none of them tell you what the first ten minutes feel like.

So let's walk through it. No mystery, no pitch.

## What happens before the first session?

Most coaches start with a short conversation or an application. This is where you both figure out whether it's a fit. They'll ask what you want help with, what you've tried, and what's going on in your life right now.

A good coach will also tell you what coaching isn't. If what you're describing sounds like it needs a therapist or a doctor, they should say so.

## What happens in a coaching session?

Sessions usually run 45 minutes to two hours. They tend to follow a rhythm like this:

**1. Landing.** You arrive, frazzled or fine. A good coach gives you a minute to actually get there. Some will start with a breath or a quick check-in.

**2. "What's most important today?"** You pick the thing. Not ten things. The one that matters most this week.

**3. Talking it through.** This is most of the session. You talk. The coach listens, reflects back what they hear and asks questions. Good questions are the whole job. "What would you do if no one would be disappointed?" "What are you actually afraid of here?" "Is this your goal, or someone else's?"

**4. The insight.** Somewhere in there, something clicks. You hear yourself say something true that you hadn't said out loud before. That's usually the moment the session was for.

**5. The next step.** You leave with one or two concrete actions. Small enough to do. Specific enough to check.

**6. Accountability.** Next time, the coach asks how it went. No shame either way, just honest curiosity. That's often where the real change happens: not in the big insight, but in showing up again.

## What does a life coach not do?

A coach doesn't tell you how to live. They don't diagnose. They don't treat mental health conditions or replace your doctor. And a good one won't do the work for you, no matter how sweetly you ask.

They also shouldn't promise results. Anyone who guarantees you'll triple your income or find your purpose in 30 days is selling you something.

## Is talking to a life coach like talking to a friend?

Sort of, with three differences.

A coach has no stake in your choices. They won't be hurt if you quit the job or move across the country.

A coach is trained to listen without jumping in with their own story.

And a coach will hold you to what you said. Friends love us too much to do that consistently.

## How is a support call different from a coaching session?

This is where a lot of people get confused, so here's the short version.

A **coaching session** is part of a longer arc. It's about change over months, with goals and follow-up.

A **support call** is about right now. At The Emotional Support Line, it's 30 minutes with one calm, trained, real human. One issue. You talk about how you feel, you settle your nervous system with someone steady on the line, and you brainstorm what to do next. Pay, pick a time, and we call you.

It's not therapy and it's not coaching. It's what you need when nothing is catastrophically wrong, but you need to talk it through. Read more about [what an emotional support call is](/blog/what-is-an-emotional-support-call).

## Try this before any session (coaching or support)

Take three minutes and write three sentences:

1. **"The thing on my mind is..."** Just name it.
2. **"What I'm feeling about it is..."** One or two words is enough.
3. **"By the end, I'd love to know..."** What would make this conversation worth it?

You'll get far more out of any conversation when you walk in knowing what you came for. And if you can't finish the sentences, that's useful too. It tells you that you need to talk before you can plan.
