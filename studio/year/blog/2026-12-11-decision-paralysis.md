"I can't make a decision. I've been going back and forth for weeks."

That's decision paralysis. You've made the pro and con list. You've asked three friends and gotten four opinions. You've lain awake running the same two scenarios. And you're no closer to a choice than when you started.

It's exhausting, and it leaks into everything. You can't fully commit to your job while you're half-deciding whether to leave it. You can't rest while a decision is sitting in your chest.

Here's a calmer way through. It takes about 20 minutes.

## Why can't I make a decision?

Usually it's not because you're indecisive by nature. It's because one or more of these is going on:

- **You're deciding while stressed.** When your body is in fight-or-flight, every option looks like a threat. You can't weigh things clearly with your shoulders up by your ears.
- **You're looking for the option with no downside.** There isn't one. Every real choice costs something.
- **You're missing one fact** and haven't noticed, so you keep circling.
- **You're trying to keep everyone happy.** That's not a decision. That's a hostage situation.

Decision paralysis is your brain trying to protect you from a mistake. The trouble is, not deciding is also a decision. It just happens slowly, and by default.

## What is the 20-minute decision method?

Set a timer. Do these in order. Don't skip the first one, even though it feels like it has nothing to do with deciding.

**Minutes 0 to 5: Settle your body.**
Put both feet on the floor. Take five slow breaths with the exhale longer than the inhale. Look around the room and name three things you can see. You're telling your nervous system you're not being chased. Decisions made from a settled body are almost always better than decisions made in a panic. (More on this in [nervous system regulation in real life](/blog/nervous-system-regulation-in-real-life).)

**Minutes 5 to 10: Write the real question.**
Not "what should I do with my life." Something you can answer yes or no to, or choose A or B. "Do I accept the offer by Friday?" "Do I tell my manager I'm stepping back from the committee?" Then write what you already know for sure, and one thing you don't know. If that unknown is something you could find out in a day, go find out before deciding.

**Minutes 10 to 15: Try each option on.**
Imagine it's six months from now and you chose Option A. Sit with it for one minute. Notice your body: does it tighten or soften? Now do the same for Option B. Your body often knows before your head admits it. Write down what you noticed, even if it's not what you expected.

**Minutes 15 to 20: Choose and name the cost.**
Pick one. Then write the sentence: "I'm choosing ___, and the cost I'm accepting is ___." Naming the cost is what makes the decision stick. You stop re-litigating it every time the downside shows up, because you already saw it coming.

Then tell one person. Saying it out loud makes it real.

## What if I choose wrong?

You might. Most decisions are more reversible than they feel at 2 am. And a "wrong" choice made clearly usually teaches you more than six months of not choosing. If it turns out badly, you'll make the next decision with better information. That's how it works for everyone.

## When should I talk it through with someone?

When you've done the thinking and you're still circling, a neutral person can help more than another list. Not someone with an opinion about the outcome. Someone calm who listens, asks good questions and helps you hear what you already know.
