New schedules. New teachers. Supply lists, forms, fees, sports sign-ups, early alarms. And somewhere in there, your kids' big feelings and your own.

Back-to-school season is a lot. Here's how to get through it with a little more ease.

## Lower the bar for the first two weeks

The first couple of weeks are a transition for everyone. Expect tired kids, short tempers and messy mornings. Plan easy dinners, protect sleep and let the house be a little chaotic. It settles.

## Make mornings easier, the night before

- Lay out clothes and pack bags in the evening
- Keep a launch pad by the door for shoes, backpacks and keys
- Prep breakfasts that take two minutes
- Set wake-up times a little earlier than you think you need

## Get the paperwork out of your head

Forms, dates, fees and reminders feed the mental load. Put everything in one place: a shared calendar, a whiteboard, a single folder. And if you have a co-parent, hand over a whole category ("you handle all sports sign-ups"), not just tasks.

## Make room for big feelings, theirs and yours

Kids often hold it together all day and fall apart at home. That's normal. A snack, quiet time and listening without fixing go a long way.

And notice your own feelings too. Many parents feel a pang of loss, worry or loneliness when kids head back. That's normal as well.

## Protect a little time for yourself

Fifteen minutes of quiet coffee after drop-off. A walk at lunch. Morning sun on the porch. You can't pour from an empty cup, especially in September.

## When it's more than a busy season

If you're feeling persistently overwhelmed, anxious or alone in carrying it all, a support call can help. Thirty minutes with a trained supporter to sort through what's on your plate and find one thing that could make it lighter.

*If you ever feel unsafe or think about suicide, please call or text 988 (US) or call 911.*

You're doing more than anyone sees.
