"How do I calm down after an argument? I'm shaking and I can't stop replaying it."

The door is closed. The call has ended. The text thread has gone quiet. And you're still in it. Heart pounding. Face hot. Writing the perfect comeback in your head for the fourteenth time.

The argument is over. Your body didn't get the memo.

## Why can't I calm down after an argument?

Because, to your body, an argument can feel like a threat. Your nervous system kicks into fight-or-flight: heart rate up, muscles tight, breath short, focus locked on the "danger."

That response doesn't switch off the second the words stop. It takes time, and it takes a few signals that you're safe now. Until then, you replay, rehearse and reheat.

This is true whether the argument was with a partner, a sibling, a parent, a coworker or a stranger in a parking lot. Your body treats them all about the same.

## What should I do right after an argument?

The first 20 minutes matter most. Here's a simple sequence we call **Cool, Then Choose**.

1. **Step away.** Physically. Another room, outside, around the block. Distance helps your body stand down.
2. **Breathe out long.** In for four, out for six to eight. At least five rounds. If your hands are shaking, let them. It's energy leaving.
3. **Move it.** Walk fast. Shake out your arms. Push against a wall. Give the fight energy somewhere to go that isn't another text.
4. **Name it.** "I'm hurt and angry." "I feel dismissed." Plain words. Not "they're impossible," just what you feel.
5. **Cool the body.** Splash cool water on your face or hold something cold. Then something warm: a mug of tea, a blanket.
6. **Wait before you choose.** No calls, texts or emails about it for at least 20 minutes. Longer if you can.

After that, you can choose what to do next with a clearer head, instead of letting adrenaline choose for you.

## How do I stop replaying the argument in my head?

Replaying is your brain trying to "win" or make sense of what happened. A few things that help:

- **Write it out, then close the notebook.** Get the whole thing on paper, every unfair word. Then stop.
- **Ask one useful question.** "What did I actually need in that moment?" The answer is usually more helpful than the comeback.
- **Change the scene.** Go outside, put on music, do something with your hands. Your brain loops less when your body is busy.
- **Talk it through with someone neutral.** Saying it out loud to a calm person often ends the loop faster than thinking about it alone.

## Should I talk to the other person right away?

Usually, not yet. Conversations go better when both people's bodies have settled.

It's okay to say, "I want to talk about this, but I need some time first. Can we come back to it tonight?" That's not avoiding. That's being responsible with your nervous system, and theirs.

When you do come back, start with one feeling and one need. "I felt dismissed. I need to feel heard about this." Short. Calm. No list of every wrong since 2019.

## Why does talking to someone else help me calm down?

Because calm is contagious. When a steady person listens to you, your body tends to settle with theirs. That's co-regulation, and it's one of the oldest ways humans calm down. (More here: [what is co-regulation?](/blog/what-is-co-regulation))

The trick is choosing the right person. Not someone who'll stoke the fire ("Oh, they did WHAT?"). Someone who'll help you cool off and think.
