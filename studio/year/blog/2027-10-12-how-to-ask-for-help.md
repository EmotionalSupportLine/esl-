You're the one people call. The organizer, the fixer, the steady one. You remember everyone's birthdays and show up with soup when someone's sick.

So who looks after you?

If you're the strong one, asking for help can feel almost impossible. But strong people need support too, maybe more than anyone.

## Why it's so hard

- **It's not your role.** People expect you to have it handled, and so do you.
- **You don't want to be a burden.**
- **It feels like weakness.**
- **It's faster to do it yourself.**
- **You're not sure anyone would say yes.**

All of this makes sense. And all of it can leave you exhausted and alone.

## Reframe what asking means

- **Asking isn't weakness.** It takes courage to admit you need something.
- **People like to help.** Many people feel honored to be asked, especially by someone who's always there for them.
- **Asking deepens relationships.** Connection grows in both directions.
- **You're modeling something important.** When the strong one asks for help, others feel freer to do the same.

## How to start asking

**Start small.** Ask for something low-stakes: a ride, a recommendation, a favor that's easy to say yes to.

**Be specific.** "Could you pick up the kids Thursday?" gets a better answer than "I could use some help."

**Ask the right person.** Choose someone who has shown they're reliable and kind.

**Let them help their way.** If you micromanage the help, you'll end up doing it yourself.

**Say thank you, not sorry.** "Thank you so much" instead of "Sorry to bother you."

**Notice how it feels.** Usually less scary than you imagined.

## Sometimes you need someone outside the circle

When you're the one everyone leans on, it can be hard to lean on the same people. A support call gives you 30 minutes with a trained, neutral person, where the focus is completely on you.

*If you're carrying something that's become too heavy, or you're thinking about suicide, please call or text 988 (US) or call 911. Reaching out is strength.*
