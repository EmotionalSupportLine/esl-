"How do I find a life coach who's actually good?"

It's January. Everybody's selling a fresh start. Your feed is full of coaches with matching sweaters promising this is finally your year.

Some of them are wonderful. Some of them took a weekend course in October. In the US, "life coach" isn't a licensed title, so anyone can use it. That's why the questions you ask before you hire matter more than the website.

Here are eight.

## What should I ask a life coach before hiring them?

**1. "What do you actually help people with?"**
"Everything" is not an answer. A good coach can tell you who they work best with and what kind of change they help with. If your thing isn't on their list, that's fine. Keep looking.

**2. "What's your training and experience?"**
There's no single required credential, so listen for substance. Where did they train? How long have they been doing this? What in their own life taught them what they teach? Lived experience counts. So does knowing their limits.

**3. "What happens in a session?"**
You should get a clear picture: how long, how often, phone or video, what you'll do between sessions. If they can't describe it plainly, they may not have a method.

**4. "What do you do when feelings come up?"**
Because they will. Real change stirs things up. You want a coach who can stay calm when you cry, without trying to turn into your therapist.

**5. "What will you do if I need more than coaching?"**
This is the big one. A good coach knows where coaching ends. If you're dealing with depression, trauma, addiction, or anything that's affecting your health, they should send you to a licensed therapist or your doctor. A coach who says they can handle anything is a coach to skip.

**6. "What's the total cost, and how do I leave?"**
Get the whole number, not just the monthly one. Ask what happens if it isn't working. Is there a review point? A notice period? You deserve to know your exit before you walk in.

**7. "Can we talk before I commit?"**
A discovery call or an application is normal. It's for both of you. Notice how you feel after. Lighter? Pressured? Your body knows.

**8. "How will we know it's working?"**
Not "you'll feel amazing." Something you can actually notice. Decisions made. Hours reclaimed. A conversation you finally had. A habit that stuck.

## What are the red flags when hiring a coach?

- **Guaranteed results.** Nobody can promise you'll double your income or find your purpose by spring.
- **"Decide today" pressure.** Countdown timers and "only two spots left at this price" every week of the year.
- **Advice to stop seeing your doctor or taking medication.** That's a hard no. Walk away.
- **Shame as a motivator.** Accountability is good. Humiliation isn't.
- **No clear way out.** If leaving sounds complicated, believe it.

## How do I know what I'm looking for?

Before you look at a single coach, get clear on your own answers. Take ten minutes with these:

1. What do I want to be different a year from now?
2. What have I already tried?
3. What gets in the way, every time?
4. How much can I spend without it adding stress?
5. Do I want someone gentle, or someone who'll call me on my stuff? (Be honest.)

If you're not sure what you need at all, start with the [2-minute quiz](/quiz). Sometimes what you need first isn't a coach.
