"How do I get better at emotional regulation?"

Maybe someone told you to work on it. Maybe you told yourself. You lost your temper, or cried in the car again, or spent a whole evening spinning about one comment, and you thought, "I need to get a grip."

Here's something most advice leaves out: emotional regulation was never meant to be a solo sport.

## What is emotional regulation?

Emotional regulation is your ability to feel your feelings without getting completely swept away by them. You still get angry, sad, scared or hurt. You just come back to steady ground more easily, and you have more choice about what you do next.

It's not about being calm all the time. It's not about pushing feelings down. All feelings are allowed. Regulation is about being able to ride the wave instead of being dragged under it.

## Why is emotional regulation so hard?

Because a lot of us were taught that calming down is something you should do quietly, privately and by yourself. "Go to your room until you can behave." "Stop crying." "Pull yourself together."

So we learned to manage alone. Or to stuff it. Or to explode and then feel ashamed.

Meanwhile, life keeps piling on: work, family, money, caregiving, the endless Busyness. Trying to regulate all of that by sheer willpower is exhausting. No wonder it doesn't stick.

## Can you regulate your emotions on your own?

Partly, yes. There are good self-regulation tools:

- Slow breathing with long exhales.
- Moving your body.
- Naming the feeling in plain words.
- Time outside and in daylight.
- Enough sleep and food (yes, being hungry counts).

These are worth practicing. Our free [10-Minute Human Reset](/free/human-reset) puts several of them together.

But there's another half to the picture.

## What is co-regulation, and how does it help?

Co-regulation is calming down *with* another person. A steady presence helps your nervous system settle. Think of how a calm friend can lower the temperature in a room just by being there, or how a toddler settles in the arms of someone relaxed.

Adults need this too. We never outgrow it. Self-regulation and co-regulation work together, and the second one often makes the first one easier. (Our full guide: [what is co-regulation?](/blog/what-is-co-regulation))

So if you've been trying to "fix" your emotions alone and feeling like a failure, consider this: maybe you've been trying to do a two-person job by yourself.

## How do I practice emotional regulation with support?

Here's a simple practice for this week. We call it **Regulate Together**.

1. **Pick your people.** Write down two or three people who feel steady to you. Not perfect. Just calm-ish and kind.
2. **Tell one of them what you're working on.** "I'm trying to get better at calming down when I'm upset. Can I text you sometimes?"
3. **Make a tiny signal.** Agree on a word or emoji that means "I'm spinning, can you talk for five minutes?"
4. **Use it once.** The first time is the hardest. Then it gets easier.
5. **Notice what helped.** Was it their voice? Their humor? Being heard without being fixed? Write it down.

If you can't think of anyone for step one, you're not broken. Plenty of capable people don't have a steady person right now. That's a gap, not a flaw.
