How to get unstuck: 5 questions that get you moving again

Wondering how to get unstuck at work or in life? These 5 honest questions help you find what's really in the way and one next step you can take today.

"How do I get unstuck?"

Maybe you typed it at 11 pm. Maybe you typed it at your desk between two meetings, staring at the same project you've been "about to start" for three weeks. Either way, you're not lazy and you're not broken. You're stuck. That's a real state, and there's a way out of it.

Here's the thing most advice gets wrong: it hands you a plan before it finds out why you're stuck. A new planner won't help if the problem is fear. A pep talk won't help if the problem is that you're exhausted. So before you do anything, ask better questions.

Why do I keep getting stuck?

Stuck isn't one thing. It's a symptom. Underneath it there's usually one of a few causes: you don't know the next step, the next step brings up a feeling you'd rather not have, you have nothing left in the tank, or you're chasing something that isn't really yours. (We go through the common ones in feeling stuck in life.)

The fix depends on the cause. That's why questions come first.

What questions help you get unstuck?

Grab a notebook or a notes app. Give yourself ten minutes. Answer these honestly, not impressively. Nobody is grading this.

1. What exactly am I stuck on? Name it in one sentence. Not "my career." Something like "I haven't updated my resume" or "I keep avoiding the budget meeting." Vague problems can't be solved. Specific ones can.

2. What's the very next physical action? Not the goal. The action. "Open the document." "Email Jen." "Block 30 minutes on Thursday." If you can't name the next action, you've found your problem: you're missing information, and your next step is to go get it.

3. What am I afraid will happen if I do it? This is the one people skip. Maybe you're afraid it won't be good enough. Maybe you're afraid someone will be disappointed, or that you'll find out the thing you wanted isn't possible. Write it down. Fears look smaller on paper than they do in your chest.

4. Do I actually have the energy for this right now? Be honest. If you're running on four hours of sleep, back-to-back meetings and a household that depends on you, the answer might be no. That's not a character flaw. It's capacity. When capacity is the issue, the next step isn't "push harder." It's "settle first." The 10-Minute Human Reset is a good place to start.

5. Whose want is this? Sometimes we're stuck because some part of us doesn't actually want the thing. It's the promotion your parents would brag about, or the goal you set five years ago when you were a different person. If the honest answer is "not mine," you're not stuck. You're being told something.

What do I do with my answers?

Look at what you wrote. One question probably hit harder than the others. That's your clue.

  • If question 2 was hard, your problem is information. Break the task down until the first step takes ten minutes or less.
  • If question 3 was hard, your problem is emotion. Say the fear out loud to someone you trust. A lot of stuck melts once the scary part has been said to another human.
  • If question 4 was hard, your problem is capacity. Drop, delegate or shrink one thing this week before you add anything.
  • If question 5 was hard, your problem is direction. Give yourself permission to want something different.

Then take one step. Just one. Movement creates clarity. You don't have to know the whole path to take the next step on it.

Can talking it through actually help?

Yes, and it's one of the fastest routes out. When you think alone, you go in circles. When you say it out loud to someone calm, you hear yourself. You notice the part you've been skipping. Your body settles a little, and a settled body thinks more clearly than a stressed one.
