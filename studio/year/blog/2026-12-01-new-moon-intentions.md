Once a month, the moon disappears from the night sky. For a night or two it's simply dark, and then a thin silver curve appears again.

People have marked that rhythm for thousands of years. You don't have to believe the moon controls anything to find it useful. It's a reliable, built-in reminder to pause, look at your life and begin again. And unlike January 1st, it comes around twelve or thirteen times a year.

## What a new moon actually is

A new moon happens when the moon sits between the Earth and the sun. The side facing us isn't lit, so we can't see it. Over the next two weeks it grows (waxes) to full, then shrinks (wanes) back to dark. One full cycle takes about 29.5 days.

The next new moon is on **December 8, 2026**, in Sagittarius.

## Why a monthly reset works better than a yearly one

Big yearly resolutions ask a lot. A month is short enough to picture and long enough to make real progress. If one month goes sideways, the next new moon is only four weeks away. There's no "I blew it, see you next year."

## A 15-minute new moon practice

You don't need candles or crystals, though you're welcome to them. You need a quiet moment, a pen and something to write on.

**1. Look back (5 minutes).** Ask yourself:
- What took most of my energy this past month?
- What am I glad I did?
- What do I want to stop carrying?

**2. Choose one intention (5 minutes).** Not ten. One. Write it as something you can actually do, in present tense:
- "I go outside for ten minutes before work."
- "I say no to one thing I don't have room for each week."
- "I call one friend every Sunday."

**3. Pick a tiny first step (5 minutes).** What's the smallest thing you could do in the next 48 hours to start? Put it in your calendar.

That's it. Fold the paper and keep it somewhere you'll see it.

## Checking in at the full moon

About two weeks later, the moon is full. That's a natural halfway point to ask: how's it going? What's working? What needs adjusting? Our post on full moon journal prompts (coming in May) goes deeper on this.
