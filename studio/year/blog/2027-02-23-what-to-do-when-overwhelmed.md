Overwhelm doesn't usually arrive with a single big problem. It's a pile-up. The email, the bill, the kid's form, the thing you said you'd do, the thing you forgot. Suddenly your chest is tight and you can't think straight.

When you're overwhelmed, the last thing you need is a 12-step plan. You need something you can do right now. Here are five, each taking about two minutes.

## 1. Stop and name it (1 minute)

Say it, out loud or in your head: *"I'm overwhelmed."* It sounds too simple, but naming what you're feeling can create a little space between you and the feeling. You're not the overwhelm. You're a person having it.

## 2. Slow your breathing (2 minutes)

Breathe in through your nose for a count of four. Breathe out slowly through your mouth for a count of six. Repeat five or six times. Many people find that a longer out-breath helps their body settle. Let your shoulders drop on each exhale.

## 3. Feel your feet (2 minutes)

Press your feet into the floor. Notice the ground holding you up. If you can, step outside and stand on grass or soil for a moment. Look at something far away: a tree, the sky, the horizon. Overwhelm narrows your focus. Looking into the distance widens it again.

## 4. Dump it on paper (3 minutes)

Write down everything in your head. Every task, worry and half-thought. Don't organize it. Just get it out. A list on paper is finite. The list in your head feels endless.

## 5. Choose one thing (2 minutes)

Look at your list and circle just one thing:

- Something that will take less than 10 minutes, or
- Something that's truly urgent today

Do only that. Then decide on the next one. You don't need to see the whole staircase, just the next step.

## Afterward: look at the pile

When you feel steadier, look at the list again with these questions:

- What can I drop completely?
- What can I ask someone else to do?
- What can wait until next week?
- What am I doing because I *should*, rather than because I need to?

Overwhelm is often a sign that you're carrying more than one person can carry. That's not a personal failing. It's a math problem.

## Talk it out

Sometimes the fastest way to untangle a pile-up is to say it all out loud to someone calm. A support call gives you 30 minutes with a real, trained supporter to sort through what's on your plate and leave with one clear next step.

*If overwhelm ever turns into feeling unsafe or thinking about suicide, please call or text 988 (US) or call 911 right away.*
