Early September has its own feeling. The light slants a little lower. The evenings come sooner. Schedules tighten up after the looser days of summer.

Many people feel a bit off during this turn of the season: more tired, more restless or a little low. That's understandable. A lot changes at once.

## Why seasonal transitions can feel hard

- **Less daylight.** The days are shortening noticeably now, and many of us get less time outside.
- **New routines.** School, work and calendars ramp back up.
- **Endings.** Summer's freedom winds down, and that can feel like a small loss.

## Ways to care for yourself through the change

**Chase the light.** Make morning light a priority as sunrise gets later. Step outside within an hour or two of waking, even for five minutes.

**Get outside every day.** Fall is a beautiful time for walks. Many people find time outdoors helps them feel steadier.

**Keep going barefoot while you can.** The ground is still warm in many places. Squeeze in a few more weeks of earthing before the cold.

**Adjust your evenings.** As darkness arrives earlier, make evenings cozy rather than dreary: soft lamps, warm drinks, a good book, earlier nights.

**Ease into new routines.** You don't have to be at full speed the first week of September.

**Nourish yourself.** Warm, seasonal foods, soups, roasted vegetables, herbal tea.

**Notice your mood.** If you tend to feel low as the days shorten, notice it early. Talk to someone. If it becomes a regular, significant pattern, it's worth mentioning to your doctor.

## A small seasonal reflection

- What did this summer give me?
- What do I want to carry into fall?
- What would make the darker months feel cozy instead of heavy?

## Keep your daily habits going

The Nest app helps with exactly this: a morning sun timer and earthing tracker with a daily streak, plus sunrise, sunset and the moon for where you are. When the seasons change, small steady habits help you change with them.
