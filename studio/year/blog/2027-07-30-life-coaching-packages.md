"I'm looking at life coaching packages and I can't tell what I'm actually paying for."

You're not imagining it. Package pages love words like "VIP," "intensive" and "transformation," and somehow never say what happens on a Tuesday.

So here's a plain guide: what coaching packages usually cost, what to check before you buy one, and a side-by-side of the three ways you can work with us.

## How much do life coaching packages cost?

Industry guides put most coaches in these ranges ([Noomii](https://orgs.noomii.com/how-much-does-a-life-coach-cost/)):

- **Newer coaches:** about $75 to $150 a session
- **Experienced coaches:** about $150 to $300 an hour
- **Specialized coaches:** $300 to $500
- **Three-month packages:** often a few thousand dollars

For more detail on what affects price, read [how much does a life coach cost](/blog/how-much-does-a-life-coach-cost).

## What should a coaching package include?

Before you buy any package, from anyone, you should be able to answer these:

- **How many hours, really?** Not "sessions." Hours.
- **Over how long?** Six sessions in six weeks is very different from six over six months.
- **What's between sessions?** Email access? Courses? Nothing?
- **What's the total cost?** The whole number, not just the first payment.
- **How do I leave?** Is there a review point? A notice period? A refund policy?
- **What if I need more than coaching?** A good coach will refer you to a licensed therapist or a doctor when that's what you need.

If the package page can't answer these, ask. If the coach can't answer them, keep looking.

## Try this: the 5-minute package test

Before you buy any coaching package, ours included, answer these on paper:

1. **What do I want to be different by the end?** One sentence.
2. **How much can I spend without it adding stress?** Money stress makes everything harder.
3. **How often do I actually want to talk to someone?** Weekly? Monthly? When it's hard?
4. **Do I want one-on-one, a group, or both?**
5. **How will I know it was worth it?** Something you could actually notice.

Your answers will usually point to one option clearly. If they point to "I'm not ready to commit to anything," that's useful too. Start small.
