For years, your days were shaped around them. School runs, sports, homework, meals, waiting up. Then one day, they're gone, to college, a job, a life of their own.

And the house is very, very quiet.

If you're feeling lost, sad or strangely unsure who you are now, you're not alone. Empty nest feelings are a normal response to one of life's biggest transitions.

## What empty nest feelings can look like

- Sadness or grief, sometimes stronger than you expected
- A sense of purpose suddenly missing
- Worry about your child's safety and happiness
- Loneliness, especially in the evenings
- Restlessness, or not knowing what to do with your time
- Relief and guilt about the relief (very common)
- Questions about your relationship now that it's "just us"

All of these are normal. Many parents feel several at once.

## Let yourself grieve the change

A season of your life has ended. Even if you're proud of your child and happy for them, you've lost a daily role that defined you for nearly two decades. It's okay to miss it.

## Rediscovering who you are

**Remember who you were before.** What did you love before you became a parent? What did you always mean to try?

**Start small.** A class, a new walking route, a hobby, a book club. Small experiments help you find what lights you up.

**Reclaim your space.** Turn a bedroom into something just for you: a studio, an office, a reading room.

**Reconnect with friends.** Many friendships fade during the busy parenting years. This is a good time to reach out.

**Build a new rhythm.** Evenings can feel the emptiest. Fill a few with plans: a walk, a call, a project.

## Your relationship with your child is changing, not ending

It becomes a relationship between adults. Try scheduled calls instead of constant texts, and let them lead. Many parents find a new kind of closeness grows over time.

## Talk it through

Empty nest feelings can be hard to explain to people who aren't going through it. A support call gives you 30 minutes with a trained supporter who will listen and help you think about what comes next for *you*.
