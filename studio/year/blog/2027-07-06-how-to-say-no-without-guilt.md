Someone asks for a favor. Before you've even thought about it, you hear yourself say, "Sure, no problem."

Then the dread kicks in.

If saying no feels almost impossible, or makes you feel guilty for days, this is for you.

## Why no is so hard

Many of us, especially women, learned early that being liked meant being helpful, agreeable and available. Saying no can feel like risking the relationship, or proving we're selfish.

But every yes has a cost. When you say yes to something you don't want, you're saying no to something else: your rest, your time, your family, your own priorities.

## Reframe what no means

- **No is information, not rejection.** You're telling someone what you can and can't do.
- **No protects your yes.** When you only say yes to what you truly want, your yes means more.
- **Guilt isn't proof you did something wrong.** It's often just the feeling of breaking an old habit.

## Simple ways to say no

**Buy time.** "Let me check and get back to you." This breaks the automatic yes.

**Keep it short.** "I can't this time." You don't have to explain.

**Offer a smaller yes, if you want to.** "I can't help with the whole event, but I can bring a dish."

**Name the conflict.** "I'd love to, but I've already committed my Saturday."

**Be warm and clear.** "Thank you for thinking of me. It's a no for now."

**For repeat askers:** "I know you're disappointed. The answer is still no."

## Getting through the guilt

The guilt usually peaks right after you say no, then fades. Try:
- Remind yourself what you protected by saying no
- Notice that the other person is (almost always) fine
- Give it a day. It usually feels lighter by tomorrow

The more you practice, the less guilt you'll feel.
