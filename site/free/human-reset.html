The 10-Minute Human Reset

For the moments when everything feels like too much. Five small steps, about ten minutes, no equipment. The last step is the one most nervous system resets leave out: another human.

Why this works
1

Name it (1 minute)

Say out loud, or write down, three words for what's happening inside you right now. "Tight. Rushed. Scared." Naming a feeling tends to turn the volume down a little. You don't have to fix it yet.

2

Breathe out longer than you breathe in (2 minutes)

Breathe in through your nose for a count of four, and out through your mouth for a count of six to eight, like blowing through a straw. Ten rounds. If counting feels like pressure, just make each exhale a little slower than the last.

3

Look around (2 minutes)

Slowly turn your head and let your eyes land on five things in the room. Name them. Then find three things you can hear. This tells your body where you are: here, now, not inside the worry.

4

Feel your feet and your weight (2 minutes)

Press your feet into the floor, or better, onto grass or earth if you can. Notice the chair holding you. Put a hand on your chest or your belly and feel it rise. Humming or a long, low "voo" sound on the exhale can feel surprisingly settling.

5

Reach for a human (3 minutes)

We calm down faster with other people. That's called co-regulation, and it's how humans are built. Text someone you trust: "Rough day. Can you send me something kind?" Call a friend and say you just need to talk for five minutes. Or book a call with a real person here.
