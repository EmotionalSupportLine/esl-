When a friend is going through something hard, a breakup, a loss, a health scare, a hard season, we want to help. But we often freeze. What do I say? What if I say the wrong thing? Should I give them space?

Here's how to show up in ways that actually help.

## Show up, even imperfectly

The biggest mistake isn't saying the wrong thing. It's disappearing because you're afraid of saying the wrong thing. A clumsy "I've been thinking about you" is better than silence.

## Listen more than you fix

When someone we love is hurting, we want to make it better. So we offer solutions, silver linings or our own stories. Most of the time, what people need first is to feel heard.

Try:
- "That sounds really hard."
- "Do you want to talk about it, or would you rather I distract you?"
- "Do you want advice, or do you just want me to listen?"

Avoid:
- "Everything happens for a reason."
- "At least…"
- "I know exactly how you feel." (Even if you've been through something similar, their experience is their own.)

## Offer specific help

"Let me know if you need anything" puts the work on them. Specific offers are easier to accept:

- "I'm dropping off dinner Thursday. Any foods you can't eat?"
- "Can I take the kids Saturday morning?"
- "I'm going to the store. What can I grab for you?"
- "Want to go for a walk tomorrow?"

## Keep showing up

Support often floods in during the first week, then disappears. Mark your calendar to check in again in two weeks, a month and three months. A simple text, "thinking of you today," can mean a lot when everyone else has moved on.

## Look after yourself too

Supporting someone through a hard time takes energy. You're allowed to have limits, to rest and to get support yourself.

## When it's more than you can carry

If your friend talks about suicide or not wanting to be alive, take it seriously. Stay with them if you can, and help them call or text **988** (US). If they're in immediate danger, call **911**. You don't have to handle that alone, and you shouldn't have to.

The most helpful thing you can be is present.
