The last week of the year has a strange, suspended feeling. Work slows down. The calendar is about to flip. It's a natural moment to look back.

But a year-end review can easily turn into a list of everything you didn't get done. That's not reflection. That's a performance review nobody asked for.

These 12 questions are designed to help you see your year clearly and kindly. Take them all at once with a cup of tea, or one a day through the first week of January.

## Looking back

**1. What were the three biggest things that happened to me this year?**
Not the biggest achievements. The biggest *things*. Some of them may have been hard.

**2. What did I handle that I didn't think I could?**
Give yourself credit here. Surviving a hard season counts.

**3. Where did my time and energy actually go?**
Look at your calendar or photos if it helps. Often the answer surprises us.

## What mattered

**5. When did I feel most like myself?**
Think of specific moments: a place, a person, an activity.

**6. Who showed up for me?**
Consider sending one of them a thank-you note this week.

**7. What did I learn the hard way?**

**8. What am I proud of that nobody else knows about?**

## Letting go

**9. What am I still carrying that I'd like to put down?**
A grudge, a regret, a role, a story about who I'm supposed to be.

**10. What do I need to forgive, in myself or someone else, to move forward?**
Forgiveness doesn't mean pretending it was okay. It can simply mean deciding it doesn't get to steer next year.

## Looking ahead

**11. What do I want more of next year? What do I want less of?**
Keep this list short and specific: "more time outside" beats "be healthier."

**12. If next year went well, how would I *feel* in December?**
Start with the feeling. Rested? Proud? Connected? Free? Then ask what would help you get there.

## Turning reflection into small habits

Big insights fade fast when daily life resumes. The trick is to turn one answer into one small daily habit.
