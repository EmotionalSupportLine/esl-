The house is quiet. The lights are off. And your mind picks this exact moment to replay that conversation from 2019, review tomorrow's to-do list and wonder whether you locked the door.

Overthinking at night is incredibly common. During the day, your mind is busy. At night, there's nothing to compete with the thoughts, so they get louder.

Here's a gentle wind-down routine that many people find helps.

## Start earlier than bedtime

A busy mind doesn't switch off the moment your head hits the pillow. The wind-down begins an hour or so before.

### 60 minutes before bed: close the day

Take five minutes to write down:
- Anything still on your mind from today
- Tomorrow's top three tasks
- Anything you're worried about

The point is to get it out of your head and onto paper, where it'll wait until morning. Many people find this "brain dump" makes it easier to let go.

### 45 minutes before bed: dim everything

Turn off overhead lights and use lamps. Lower your screen brightness or put the phone away. Your surroundings tell your body what time it is.

### 30 minutes before bed: do something slow

A warm shower, gentle stretching, a few pages of a calm book, a cup of caffeine-free tea, quiet music. Choose something that doesn't demand much of your mind.

## When the thoughts come anyway

**Don't fight them.** Trying hard *not* to think usually makes thoughts louder. Notice the thought, name it ("there's the work worry again"), and let it drift by like a car passing outside.

**Breathe longer out than in.** In for four, out for six. Repeat until your body softens.

**Do a body scan.** Starting at your toes, slowly notice each part of your body, letting it relax as you go.

**Get up if you need to.** If you've been lying awake a long time, get up, keep the lights low, and do something quiet until you feel sleepy.

## Daytime habits that help nighttime

What happens during the day matters too. Many people find these help them sleep:
- Getting outside in morning light
- Spending time outdoors and moving their body
- Keeping caffeine to the morning

## When it's more than a busy mind

If you've been sleeping badly for weeks, or worry is affecting your days, it's worth talking to your doctor. And if you'd like to talk through what's keeping you up, a support call can help untangle it.

## Build the habits

The Nest app helps with the daytime side: a morning sun timer, earthing time and a daily streak, plus sunrise and sunset for where you are. Small daytime habits that many people find make evenings easier.
