## The easiest way to start

Make a cup of tea, open the photos on your phone and scroll back to January. Write down one thing for every few photos: a trip, a hard conversation, a birthday you pulled off, a week you got through.

The first 20 are the hardest. After that, it starts to pour out.

# How far did you get?

Hi FNAMETAG,

If you’re stuck somewhere around 20, you’re right on schedule. Most people are. Here are a few places to look:

- **Your bank statements.** Every payment is a story: the class you signed up for, the car you fixed, the gift you chose.
- **Your calendar.** Appointments you kept, people you showed up for, things you finally scheduled.
- **The invisible work.** Meals planned. Laundry done. Someone you looked after. It counts. All of it.
- **What you survived.** A loss, a move, a diagnosis in the family, a job that drained you. Getting through it is on the list.

And one question from the workbook to sit with today: *What stressed you out in January that doesn’t stress you out now?*

Keep going. You did more than you remember.

# The year I was told I’d done “nothing”

Hi FNAMETAG,

When I had a baby and a preschooler, my then-husband had the nerve to tell me I’d been doing “nothing.”

So I sat down and made a list. Keeping two small children alive. Breastfeeding. Teaching a preschooler to read. Mountains of laundry. Starting a business and a blog. Substitute teaching. I came up with well over 100 things.

He was unmoved. But I felt like a total badass.

I’ve done this list every year since, and I’ve never met anyone who didn’t do at least 100 things. We just forget. By November we’ve moved on to the next big thing and February is a blur.

When you finish your list, read it out loud. Then give yourself a Free Pass for what didn’t get done this year. Really. Let it go.
