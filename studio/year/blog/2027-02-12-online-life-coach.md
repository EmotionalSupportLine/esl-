"I want an online life coach. But does it actually work through a screen?"

It's a reasonable worry. You're picturing a frozen video, a lag every time you start crying, and a dog barking in the background at the exact moment you finally say the true thing.

Here's the honest answer: online coaching works well for a lot of people. Sometimes better than in person. And it works best when it's paired with something most coaching leaves out: real-time emotional support.

## Is online life coaching as good as in person?

For most coaching, yes. Coaching is a conversation. It's listening, questions, reflection and follow-through. None of that needs a shared room.

Online has some real advantages:

- **No commute.** You don't lose an hour driving to a 50-minute session.
- **Your own space.** You can be in sweatpants, under a blanket, with your tea. People are often more honest at home.
- **More choice.** You're not limited to the coaches in your zip code. You can find the one who actually fits.
- **Easier to keep going.** When it's easy to show up, you keep showing up. That's where change lives.

The one thing to watch: it's easier to hide on a screen. Camera off, half-listening, checking email in another tab. Online coaching asks you to actually be there.

## Phone or video: which is better?

Whichever lets you relax.

Video helps some people feel connected. Others find it exhausting, like being on a work call about their feelings. Phone can feel surprisingly intimate. No one's watching your face. You can walk, lie down, look out a window.

Our support calls are phone calls. We call you. You don't need an app, a link or a decent hair day.

## Why pair online coaching with emotional support?

Here's what coaching programs don't always tell you. You can have the best plan in the world, and when your nervous system is in fight-or-flight, you won't follow it.

Coaching is built for change over months. But life doesn't wait for your next session. The hard conversation with your sister happens on a Tuesday. The work email that makes your stomach drop shows up at 3 pm. Your coach is booked for two weeks from now.

That's where emotional support comes in. A calm, steady human on the line, right when it's hard, helps your body settle. That's called co-regulation: your nervous system borrowing steadiness from someone else's. (More on that in [what is co-regulation](/blog/what-is-co-regulation).) Once you're settled, you can think again. Then the plan works.

So the pairing looks like this:

- **Coaching** for the long arc: goals, decisions, the big picture.
- **Emotional support** for the moments in between: when it's hard right now and you need to talk it through.

## What should I look for in an online life coach?

- They can describe plainly what happens in a session.
- They know where coaching ends and therapy begins, and they'll tell you if you need a licensed therapist or a doctor.
- They're clear about cost, schedule and how to leave.
- They don't promise guaranteed results.
- After a first conversation, you feel more like yourself, not less.

## Try this: set up your space for an online session

Small things make a big difference. Before your next call (any kind), take five minutes:

1. **Close every other tab.** Seriously. All of them.
2. **Tell the house.** "I'm on a call for 30 minutes. Unless something's on fire, it can wait."
3. **Get comfortable.** Water or tea. A blanket. Shoes off.
4. **Feel your feet.** Press them into the floor for three slow breaths, with long exhales.
5. **Write one line.** "What I most want to talk about is..."

That's it. You'll arrive. And arriving is half of it.
