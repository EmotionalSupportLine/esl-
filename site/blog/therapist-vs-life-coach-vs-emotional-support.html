Therapist vs life coach vs emotional support: what kind of help do I need?

Life coach or therapist? Or do you just need someone to talk to? A plain-English guide to what each kind of help does, what it costs, and how to choose what fits right now.

You know you need something. But what? A therapist? A life coach? A friend? A crisis line?

These are different jobs, and choosing the right one saves you time, money and a lot of frustration. Here's a plain-English guide.

The quick answer

I need... Consider
Diagnosis or treatment of a mental health condition A licensed therapist or your doctor
Help right now because I'm not safe A crisis line: call or text 988 (US) or 911
Structured change toward a goal over months A coach
Financial planning or investment advice A qualified financial professional
Someone to listen and help me think something through Emotional support, like The Emotional Support Line
Help settling down and sorting out what I need next Emotional support
Ongoing human support plus personal growth tools A membership like the Nest

What does a therapist do?

A therapist is a licensed mental health professional. Therapy is the right choice when you're dealing with depression, anxiety that disrupts daily life, trauma, addiction, or anything that needs assessment and treatment. Therapists can diagnose, and they're trained for deep, long-term work. Insurance sometimes covers it, and there are often waiting lists.

What does a life coach do?

A life coach helps you move toward a goal: a career change, a business, a habit, a big decision. Coaching looks forward. It's structured, usually runs for weeks or months, and is about action and accountability. Coaches don't diagnose or treat. Prices vary widely, from around $75 to $300 or more a session (see how much a life coach costs).

What is emotional support?

Emotional support is the human kind of help most of us actually need most often: someone calm who listens, reflects back what they hear, and helps you find your footing. It's for the in-between, when nothing is catastrophically wrong, but you need to talk it through.

At The Emotional Support Line, that looks like a 30-minute call: one issue, one real person, one clear next step. It's not therapy, and it's not a crisis line. It's talking about feelings, brainstorming solutions and settling your nervous system, with a person.

How is emotional support different from therapy?

We're not therapy, on purpose:

  • We don't touch your insurance or your employer's plan. No diagnosis, no claim, nothing on your record.
  • We won't make you talk about your mother for years. The past matters, but you don't have to excavate it to feel better this week.
  • We're pragmatic. First we help you get out of fight-or-flight, because nobody thinks clearly while their alarm is going off. Then we co-regulate: humans calm down with humans, and if you don't have people in your life for that right now, that's what we're here for. Then we brainstorm a practical next step you can take with a steadier nervous system.

Therapy is the right fit for diagnosing and treating mental health conditions and for deep trauma work. If what we hear sounds like it needs that, we'll tell you.

Is a life coach worth it? Is therapy worth it?

Both are worth it when they fit the job. Coaching is worth paying for when you have a clear goal and need structure. Therapy is worth it when something is affecting your health or your ability to function.

Neither is a great fit when what you really need is to be heard this week. That's where a single support call often does more, for a lot less.

Can I use more than one?

Yes, and many people do. Some of our callers see a therapist monthly and call us between sessions when something comes up. Some work with a coach and use a call to untangle the feelings that keep getting in the way of the plan.
