On **Saturday, March 20, 2027**, we reach the spring equinox in the Northern Hemisphere. For one day, daylight and darkness are nearly equal. After that, the days grow longer than the nights until the summer solstice in June.

It's also the start of the astrological new year, as the sun moves into Aries.

## What the equinox is

Twice a year, the sun sits directly over the equator. On those days, almost every place on Earth gets roughly 12 hours of daylight and 12 hours of night. *Equinox* comes from the Latin for "equal night."

The spring equinox marks the shift from winter's long nights toward summer's long days. Many cultures have celebrated it as a time of renewal, planting and fresh starts.

## A balance check-in

The equinox is a natural moment to ask how balanced your own life feels. Take 15 minutes with a pen and rate each area from 1 (very out of balance) to 5 (feels good):

- **Rest:** am I getting enough sleep and real downtime?
- **Work:** does my work feel sustainable?
- **People:** do I feel connected to people who care about me?
- **Body:** am I moving, eating and getting outside in ways that feel good?
- **Joy:** when did I last do something just because I enjoy it?
- **Me:** do I have any time that's truly my own?

Then ask:

1. **Which area is lowest?**
2. **What's one small change that would raise it by just one point?**

Not a total overhaul. One point. That's how balance actually comes back: gradually, like the light.

## Ways to mark the equinox

- Watch the sunrise or sunset, and notice how much later it is than in December
- Spend time outside: a walk, a garden, a park bench
- Clear one small space in your home, a drawer or a shelf, as a symbolic spring clean
- Plant something, even herbs in a pot on the windowsill
- Write one intention for the coming season
