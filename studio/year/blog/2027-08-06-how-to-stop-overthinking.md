"How do I stop overthinking? I've been going over the same thing for days and I'm exhausted."

You replay the conversation. You draft the text eleven times. You run every possible outcome, then run them again. It feels like you're working on the problem. You're not. You're spinning.

Here's how to get out of the loop, especially during the day, when you need your brain for other things.

## Why do I overthink everything?

Overthinking usually isn't a thinking problem. It's a safety problem.

When something feels uncertain or risky, your nervous system goes on alert. Your brain tries to protect you by thinking ahead: what if this, what if that. A little of that is useful. But when your body stays on alert, your brain keeps searching for an answer that will make you feel safe. And there often isn't one, because the future is uncertain no matter how much you think about it.

So the loop keeps going. Not because you're weak or indecisive, but because your body hasn't gotten the "all clear."

That's why thinking harder rarely fixes overthinking. Settling down first usually does. (More on that in [nervous system regulation in real life](/blog/nervous-system-regulation-in-real-life).)

## What are the signs I'm overthinking, not problem-solving?

Here's a quick way to tell:

| Problem-solving | Overthinking |
|---|---|
| New information shows up | The same thoughts repeat |
| You get closer to a choice | You get further from one |
| You feel a bit calmer after | You feel more tense after |
| It has an end | It goes until you're exhausted |

If you're in the right column, more thinking won't help. Something else will.

## How do I stop overthinking right now?

Try this four-step way out. It takes about ten minutes.

**1. Settle your body.** Put both feet on the floor. Breathe in for four, out for six or eight. Do that five times. Look around and name five things you can see. You're telling your nervous system it's safe enough to stop scanning for danger.

**2. Get it out of your head.** Write down every thought in the loop, as fast as you can. No editing. Thoughts on paper stop circling, because your brain no longer has to hold them.

**3. Find the real question.** Look at the page. Underneath all of it, what is the actual decision or worry? Write it in one sentence. "Should I tell my boss I'm overwhelmed?" "Is my friend mad at me?"

**4. Choose a next step, not a final answer.** You don't have to solve the whole thing. Just pick the next small, real action. Send the text. Make the call. Ask the question. Or decide on purpose to revisit it Thursday at 4 pm, and put it on your calendar.

Action breaks loops. Even small action.

## What if I'm afraid of making the wrong decision?

That fear is behind a lot of overthinking. A few things to remember:

- **Most decisions can be adjusted.** Very few choices are permanent.
- **Not deciding is also a decision.** Waiting has costs, too.
- **Your body knows things.** When you imagine each option, notice which one makes your chest open and which one makes it tighten. That's information.
- **You're allowed to be wrong.** You'll learn something either way.

## When is overthinking more than overthinking?

If your thoughts feel out of control, are full of constant fear or dread, or are keeping you from sleeping, working or enjoying life for weeks at a time, please talk to your doctor or a licensed therapist. Persistent anxiety is real, treatable, and deserves real care.

And if your thoughts turn dark: if you're in danger or thinking about suicide, call or text 988 (US).
