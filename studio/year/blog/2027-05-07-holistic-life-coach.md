"I want a holistic life coach. Someone who looks at my whole life, not just one piece."

Because you've tried the one-piece approach.

You made a budget, and then a stressful month blew it up. You made a schedule, and then your body crashed and you lost a week. You tried to "work on your feelings," but the money worry was so loud you couldn't hear anything else.

That's not a failure of willpower. That's what happens when you treat a connected system like a bunch of separate problems.

## What is a holistic life coach?

A holistic life coach looks at your life as one whole thing. Instead of only working on your career, or only your money, they ask how all the parts are affecting each other.

"Holistic" just means "whole." It doesn't mean crystals-only or anti-doctor. A good holistic coach works alongside your doctor, not instead of them, and will send you to a licensed therapist or a financial professional when that's what you need.

## Why aren't money, time, body and feelings separate?

Because they're all running on the same nervous system: yours.

Watch how they feed each other:

- **Money stress lands in your body.** Tight chest, bad sleep, clenched jaw. Then you're too tired to make good money decisions.
- **No time means no body care.** You skip the walk, the water, the sunlight. Then everything feels harder.
- **An exhausted body shrinks your patience.** Then small feelings turn into big ones.
- **Unfelt feelings drive spending and busyness.** The online cart at midnight. The yes you didn't mean. The extra project so you don't have to sit still.

Pull one thread and the whole sweater moves. That's actually good news. It means one small change in the right place can loosen everything else.

## Where should I start if everything feels connected?

Start with your body. Not because it matters most, but because it's the fastest way in.

When your nervous system is in fight-or-flight, you can't think clearly about money, time or anything else. Settle first, then decide. That's the whole idea behind [nervous system regulation in real life](/blog/nervous-system-regulation-in-real-life).

Then look for the corner that's loudest right now, and work there.

## Try this: the four-corner check-in

Take five minutes. Rate each of these from 1 (struggling) to 10 (good) for the past week:

1. **Money.** Not your bank balance. How you *feel* about money right now.
2. **Time.** How much of your week felt like it belonged to you?
3. **Body.** Sleep, movement, time outside, how you feel physically.
4. **Emotional life.** How much room you've had to feel what you feel and talk about it.

Now:

- **Circle the lowest number.** That's your starting corner.
- **Pick one small action for that corner this week.** Ten minutes outside in the morning light. One money conversation you've been avoiding. One hour on the calendar that's yours. One call with someone who'll actually listen.
- **Check again in a week.** Notice which other corners moved too. They usually do.
