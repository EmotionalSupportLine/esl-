"What is a life coach, anyway?"

Fair question. The title gets used for everything from career strategy to crystal readings. Your cousin's friend is one. Your old boss is one now. Somebody on Instagram is one with a ring light and a waitlist.

So here's the plain version, with no sales pitch hiding in it.

## What is a life coach, in simple terms?

A life coach is someone who helps you get from where you are to where you want to be. They help you get clear on what you want, notice what's in the way, make a plan, and actually follow through.

Coaching looks forward. It's less "why am I like this?" and more "okay, so what now?"

A good coach listens hard, asks the question you've been avoiding, and won't let you off the hook when you say "I'll start Monday" for the ninth Monday in a row.

## What does a life coach help with?

People hire coaches for all kinds of things:

- A career change, or figuring out whether to make one
- Starting or growing a business
- Getting control of their time
- Changing their relationship with money (the feelings, not the investments)
- A big decision they keep circling
- Rebuilding after a divorce, a move, an empty nest or a loss
- Feeling like they've lost themselves in everyone else's needs

The common thread: you want something to change, and you want help making it happen.

## What doesn't a life coach do?

This part matters, so let's be clear.

A life coach is not a therapist. Coaches don't diagnose, and they don't treat depression, anxiety disorders, trauma or addiction. If something is affecting your health or your ability to get through the day, a licensed therapist or your doctor is the right call.

A life coach is not a financial advisor. A coach can help with the feelings and habits around money. Investment, tax and debt decisions belong with a qualified financial professional.

A life coach is not a crisis line, and neither are we. (Crisis help is at the end of this post.)

If you want the full side-by-side, read [therapist vs life coach vs emotional support](/blog/therapist-vs-life-coach-vs-emotional-support).

## Do life coaches need a license?

In the US, "life coach" isn't a licensed title. That means anyone can use it. Some coaches have serious training and years of experience. Some took a weekend course. Some have neither.

That's not a reason to avoid coaching. It's a reason to ask good questions before you hire someone: What's your training? What happens in a session? How do I leave if it isn't working? What do you do if I need more help than coaching?

A good coach will be glad you asked.

## How much does a life coach cost?

Prices vary a lot. Industry guides put most coaches in these ranges ([Noomii](https://orgs.noomii.com/how-much-does-a-life-coach-cost/)):

- **Newer coaches:** about $75 to $150 a session
- **Experienced coaches:** about $150 to $300 an hour
- **Specialized coaches:** $300 to $500
- **Three-month packages:** often a few thousand dollars

For more on what you get at each price, see [how much does a life coach cost](/blog/how-much-does-a-life-coach-cost).

## Is a life coach the same as emotional support?

Not quite, and this is where a lot of people spend money they didn't need to.

Coaching is built for change over months. Emotional support is built for right now. Sometimes you don't need a twelve-week plan. You need one calm human to listen for half an hour while you say the thing out loud, so your body can settle down and your brain can come back online.

Both are good. They're just different jobs.

## Try this: the "what kind of help" check

Grab a pen and answer these three questions honestly:

1. **Is there a specific thing I want to change in the next 6 to 12 months?** If yes, that's coaching territory.
2. **Do I mostly need to be heard this week?** If yes, start with emotional support.
3. **Is this affecting my sleep, my health, or my ability to function for weeks now?** If yes, please start with your doctor or a licensed therapist.

You might answer yes to more than one. That's normal. Start with whichever feels most urgent.
