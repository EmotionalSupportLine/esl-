"Vagus nerve exercises." You've probably seen them all over your feed: humming, cold water, gargling, eye movements, someone very calm in a very clean kitchen.

And maybe you thought, "Okay, but do I need to buy something? Am I doing it wrong? Is this real?"

Happy New Year. Let's keep it simple and honest.

## What is the vagus nerve?

The vagus nerve is a real, long nerve that runs from your brainstem down through your neck and chest into your belly. It connects your brain with your heart, lungs and digestive system, and it plays a part in the "rest and digest" side of your nervous system.

That's the plain anatomy. Beyond that, a lot of what you read online goes further than the science does. Researchers are still studying how much breathing, humming and the rest affect the vagus nerve directly.

So here's how we think about it: the practices people call "vagus nerve exercises" are mostly old, gentle calming habits. Many people find them settling. They're low-risk, free and worth trying. You don't need to believe any big theory for them to be useful.

## Do vagus nerve exercises work?

Honest answer: they're not a cure, a treatment or a guarantee. They won't fix a hard situation.

What they can do is help you feel a bit calmer in the moment, which is often enough to think more clearly and choose your next move instead of reacting. That's a good deal for a few minutes and zero dollars.

If you have a heart condition, a breathing condition, or any medical concern, check with your doctor before trying anything new, especially cold water or breath holds.

## What are some easy vagus nerve exercises?

Here are five low-stakes ones. Pick one. You don't need all five.

**1. The long exhale.**
Breathe in through your nose for a count of four. Breathe out slowly through your mouth for a count of six to eight. Do it five to ten times. The longer out-breath is the part most people find calming.

**2. Humming.**
Close your mouth and hum one long, low note on your out-breath. Feel the buzz in your chest and face. Do it three to five times. Bonus: it's very hard to stay furious while humming.

**3. Cool water on your face.**
Splash cool (not icy) water on your face, or hold a cool, damp cloth over your eyes and cheeks for 20 to 30 seconds. A lot of people find this snaps them out of a spiral. Skip it if cold bothers you.

**4. Slow look-around.**
Let your eyes move slowly around the room, left to right. Turn your head gently with them. Name a few things you see. This is a simple way of telling your body, "I checked. We're okay."

**5. Feet on the earth.**
Stand outside for a few minutes, ideally barefoot on grass or soil if the weather allows. Feel your weight drop into your feet. Look at the sky. (In January, a coat and a few minutes of daylight on the porch counts.) Our Nest members track this in Earth Charge, but your own two feet work just fine.

## How often should I do vagus nerve exercises?

Little and often beats big and rare. Try one practice for two minutes, once or twice a day, and again when you feel your shoulders creeping toward your ears.

If it helps, tie it to something you already do. Long exhales while the coffee brews. A hum in the car before you walk into work. A splash of water after a hard phone call.
