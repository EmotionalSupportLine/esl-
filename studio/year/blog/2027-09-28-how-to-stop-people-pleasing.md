You say yes when you mean no. You apologize for things that aren't your fault. You can sense the slightest shift in someone's mood and immediately try to fix it. You'd rather be uncomfortable than make anyone else uncomfortable.

That's people-pleasing. And if you recognize yourself, you're in very good company.

## Where it comes from

People-pleasing usually starts as a smart strategy. Somewhere along the way, often in childhood, you learned that keeping others happy kept you safe, loved or out of trouble. Many women were also taught directly that being agreeable and accommodating was what made them "good."

The strategy made sense then. The problem is that it keeps running long after it stops serving you.

## The hidden cost

- Exhaustion from managing everyone's feelings
- Resentment that builds quietly
- Losing track of what *you* actually want
- Relationships where people don't really know you
- A life that's full, but not yours

## How to start loosening its grip

**1. Notice the moment.** Catch the instant before an automatic yes. Even just noticing it is progress.

**2. Pause before answering.** "Let me get back to you" creates space to check in with yourself.

**3. Ask what you want.** Not what you should want or what would make them happy. What do you want?

**4. Practice small disappointments.** Order what you actually want. Leave when you're ready to leave. Share a real opinion. Small reps build the muscle.

**5. Let people have their feelings.** Someone else's disappointment isn't an emergency you have to fix. You can be kind and still let them feel it.

**6. Expect discomfort.** Changing a lifelong pattern feels awkward and guilty at first. That's normal. It gets easier.
