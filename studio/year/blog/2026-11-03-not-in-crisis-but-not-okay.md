There's a strange, lonely place a lot of people live in. You're not in danger. You're getting up, going to work, feeding people, answering texts. From the outside, you're fine.

But you're not okay either.

Maybe your chest is tight most of the day. Maybe you cry in the car. Maybe you've stopped looking forward to anything. And because nothing is technically "wrong," you tell yourself you don't have the right to ask for help.

This post is for the in-between.

## Your struggle counts, even if it isn't an emergency

We tend to think help is only for big moments: a crisis, a health scare, a loss everyone can see. So we wait. We wait until it gets worse, as if we need to earn support by falling apart completely.

You don't have to wait. Feeling heavy, stuck or quietly overwhelmed is a real reason to reach out. Small problems that get attention early tend to stay smaller.

## Signs you're in the in-between

You might recognize a few of these:

- You feel flat, tired or on edge most days, and you can't say exactly why
- You keep saying "I'm fine" and hearing how hollow it sounds
- Little things make you snap or cry
- You've stopped calling friends because you don't want to be "a downer"
- You lie awake replaying the same conversation or worry
- You're carrying something you haven't said out loud to anyone

None of this means something is wrong with you. It means you're human, and you've been carrying a lot.

## What helps, starting today

**Say it out loud to one person.** Not a full confession. Just one honest sentence: "I've been having a really hard time lately." Many people find that naming a feeling to a calm listener takes some of its weight away.

**Lower the bar for the day.** In the in-between, ordinary tasks cost more energy. Pick the three things that truly must happen today and let the rest wait.

**Get outside for ten minutes.** Step outside, look at the sky, put your feet on grass if you can. It won't fix everything. It's a small reset that asks very little of you.

**Write down what's actually on your mind.** Worries feel bigger when they're spinning in your head. On paper, they become a list you can look at.

**Notice what's yours to carry.** A lot of heaviness comes from holding other people's feelings and problems. Ask yourself: what part of this is actually mine?

## When to talk to someone

If you've been feeling this way for a while, it can help to talk it through with someone outside your life. That means someone who isn't your partner, your mother or your best friend. Someone neutral, who can listen without needing anything back.

That's exactly what our support calls are for. They're 30 minutes with a real, trained person (never AI), focused on one thing that's weighing on you, so you leave a little steadier and clearer about your next step.

## If it ever becomes more than the in-between

If you start thinking about ending your life, or you feel you might hurt yourself or someone else, that's no longer the in-between. Please call or text **988** in the US (the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), or call **911**. They're trained for exactly this, any hour of the day.

For everything short of that, you're allowed to ask for help now. You don't have to wait until it's worse.
