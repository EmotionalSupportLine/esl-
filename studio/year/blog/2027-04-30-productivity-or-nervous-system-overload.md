"I think I need a productivity coach. I'm getting less done than ever."

Maybe you used to be the person who got it all done. Now you sit down to work and drift. You reread the same paragraph three times. You open a tab, forget why, open another. By 4 pm you're fried and you've done half of what you planned.

A productivity coach might help. But before you hire one, it's worth asking a different question: is this a productivity problem, or is your nervous system overloaded?

They look alike from the outside. They need very different fixes.

## What does a productivity coach do?

A productivity coach helps you organize your time, tasks and habits so you get more of the right things done. They might help you set priorities, design a weekly routine, cut distractions or build systems. For the right person, it's great. If you have the energy and just need better methods, a good productivity coach can make a real difference.

## What is nervous-system overload?

Your nervous system is always adjusting to what's around you. When you're under steady pressure, like long days, constant pings, caregiving, money stress or a hard season at work, it can stay revved up for a long time without a real break.

When that goes on, you may notice it gets harder to focus, harder to switch tasks, harder to remember things and harder to rest. Everything takes more effort. Not because you've lost your skills, but because your system is spending its energy on staying on alert.

No productivity system can fix that, because the problem isn't your method. It's your fuel.

## How do I tell the difference?

Here's a quick side-by-side.

**It's more likely a productivity problem if:**
- You feel basically okay, just disorganized.
- You lose time to distraction, not exhaustion.
- You have a few free hours you aren't using well.
- New systems help and stick.

**It's more likely nervous-system overload if:**
- You feel tired and wired at the same time.
- Small tasks feel huge.
- You can't rest even when you have the time.
- You snap at people more than usual, or feel numb.
- New systems help for a week, then collapse.
- It got worse after a stretch of stress, not after you "got lazy."

Our post on [why you can't get anything done](/blog/why-cant-i-get-anything-done) goes deeper into the capacity side of this.

## What helps with nervous-system overload?

The fix is less about doing more and more about recovering enough to do anything well.

**Settle your body a few times a day.** Not once a week at yoga. A few minutes, several times a day. Slow breathing with long exhales. Feet on the floor. Looking out a window. These small moments add up.

**Get outside, especially in the morning.** Many people find that morning light and a bit of time outdoors help them feel steadier through the day. The Earth Charge tools in the Nest help you track this without thinking about it.

**Cut something.** Drop, hand off or shrink one commitment this week. Overload doesn't get better with better scheduling. It gets better with less.

**Talk to a calm person.** Being with someone steady helps your body settle faster than doing it alone. That's co-regulation, and it's one of the most underrated tools for overloaded professionals.

**Then, if you still want it, get productivity help.** Once you've got some fuel back, systems start working again.

## Try this: the 10-minute test

Before you hire anyone or buy another planner, try this for one week.

1. Every morning before you open your laptop, do the [10-Minute Human Reset](/free/human-reset): name what you feel, take long exhales, look around, feel your feet, reach for a human.
2. Notice your focus for the first hour after.
3. At the end of the week, ask: did my work get easier?

If it did, even a little, overload is part of the picture. Keep resetting, and start cutting.
