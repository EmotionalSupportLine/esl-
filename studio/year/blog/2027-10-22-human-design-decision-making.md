"I looked up my Human Design authority. Now what do I actually do with it?"

Good. Because the point was never to memorize a label.

Most of us were taught to decide one way: make a pros and cons list, think it through, do what's sensible, do what people expect. Some of us were taught that our own wants don't count at all.

That's conditioning. It's not who you are. And your Human Design authority is one of the most useful ways to start noticing the difference.

## What is Human Design authority?

In Human Design, your **authority** describes how you're built to make decisions. Not what to decide. *How.*

The main ones, very briefly:

- **Emotional:** clarity comes over time, after the feeling wave passes. Sleep on it.
- **Sacral:** a gut "uh-huh" or "uh-uh" in the moment.
- **Splenic:** a quiet, instant knowing that speaks once.
- **Ego (Heart):** "Do I really want this? Will I put my heart into it?"
- **Self-Projected:** clarity comes from hearing yourself talk it through.
- **Mental (Environmental):** clarity comes from talking with trusted people in the right setting.
- **Lunar:** for Reflectors, clarity comes over about a month.

You can see yours on a [free Human Design chart](/#chart) in about a minute.

## Isn't this just a chart telling me what to do?

No. And this matters.

Tracee's take: Human Design isn't a new set of rules to obey. It's a mirror that helps you see where you've been living as who society told you to be. Then it gives you permission to stop.

Your authority is a way to test that. Every time you use it, you're practicing trusting yourself over the voice that says "be reasonable," "don't make waves," "what will people think?"

Learn who you are. Explore it. Then stop apologizing for it, and be it.

(As always, Human Design is for reflection and insight, not prediction. It doesn't replace medical, legal or financial advice.)

## How does Human Design help with real-life decisions?

Here's what it can look like in everyday life.

**The invitation.** You get asked to lead another committee. Your conditioning says yes before the sentence is even over. If you have Sacral authority, you pause and notice your gut. Was that an "uh-huh" or an "uh-uh"? If you have Emotional authority, you say, "Let me get back to you tomorrow," and you mean it.

**The job question.** Work is changing fast, and a lot of people are wondering whether to stay put, retrain or try something new. That's exactly when conditioning gets loud: "Stay safe. You have a degree. Don't throw it away." Your authority won't tell you the answer. It will help you hear your own answer under the noise.

**The purchase.** Splenic authority might give you a quiet "no" in the moment that your mind talks you out of. Ego authority asks whether you really want it, or just think you should.

**The big move.** Self-Projected authority needs to say it out loud to someone who'll just listen, without advice. Hearing yourself is the point.

## Why is deconditioning so hard?

Because the old way kept you safe and approved of for a long time. Your Ego will argue. Your family might be confused. You might feel guilty the first few times you decide your own way.

That's normal. Deconditioning isn't a one-day event. It's a lot of small moments where you choose yourself, and then don't apologize for it.

## Try this: a two-week decision log

Keep a simple note on your phone for two weeks. For each decision, big or small, write:

1. **The decision.** "Whether to go to the reunion."
2. **What my conditioning said.** "You have to. People will talk."
3. **What my authority said.** Gut response, feeling after a night's sleep, hearing yourself talk, whatever yours is.
4. **What I chose.**
5. **How I felt a day later.** Lighter? Heavier?

At the end of two weeks, read it back. Notice which choices felt like you.
