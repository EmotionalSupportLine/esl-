On **Saturday, February 20, 2027**, the full moon passes through the outer edge of the Earth's shadow. It's a penumbral lunar eclipse.

It's a subtle one: the moon won't turn red or disappear, it'll just look slightly shaded on one side. But eclipses have been watched and wondered about for as long as humans have looked up, and this one comes at the end of an eclipse season that started with an annular solar eclipse on February 6.

## What a lunar eclipse is

A lunar eclipse happens when the Earth sits directly between the sun and the full moon, so the Earth's shadow falls on the moon.

The Earth's shadow has two parts: a dark inner core (the umbra) and a lighter outer edge (the penumbra). In a **penumbral** eclipse like this one, the moon only passes through the lighter outer shadow. That's why it's so subtle. It's best seen from Europe, Africa and Asia. From most of North America, the moon rises around the time the eclipse is ending, so the shading may be too faint to notice. That's fine: the full moon itself is still worth stepping outside for.

## Eclipse season, in astrology

Eclipses come in pairs or threes about every six months, a couple of weeks apart. This season brings an annular solar eclipse on **February 6** (new moon in Aquarius) and this lunar eclipse on **February 20** (full moon in Virgo).

In astrology, eclipses are often described as moments of change, endings and beginnings, like a cosmic reset. We think of them as a natural prompt to notice what's shifting in your life. Not a prediction. Just a pause.

## A full moon eclipse reflection

Full moons are traditionally a time for completion and release. The full moon in Virgo invites a look at the everyday: your routines, your health habits, your work and the small details.

Take 15 minutes on or around February 20 with these questions:

1. **What has come to a natural end lately, even if I haven't admitted it yet?**
2. **What daily habit is quietly draining me?**
3. **What small, practical change would make my ordinary days feel better?**
4. **What am I ready to release, so something new has room?**

Write freely. You don't have to act on anything yet. Eclipses are better for noticing than for forcing.

## Step outside if you can

If the sky is clear and the moon is up where you are, go out and look. Even if you can't see the shading, standing under a full moon for a few minutes is its own small ritual.
