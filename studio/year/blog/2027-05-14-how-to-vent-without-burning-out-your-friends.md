"I need to vent. But I've been venting to the same two people for months and I can feel them getting tired."

Venting is good. Getting something off your chest can lower the pressure fast. But there's venting that helps and venting that just goes in circles, and the second kind can wear out you and your friends.

Here's how to do the first kind.

## Is venting actually good for you?

It can be. Saying a feeling out loud to someone who listens can help your body come down from stress. Feeling heard matters. Being alone with a hard feeling is often worse than the feeling itself.

But venting has a shelf life. When it turns into retelling the same story over and over, adding new outrage each time, it can actually keep you stirred up instead of settling you down. The goal isn't to get rid of the feeling by yelling it into the void. It's to move through it and come out the other side a little clearer.

## How do I vent in a healthy way?

Here are five simple rules.

**1. Ask first.** "Do you have a few minutes for me to vent?" gives your friend the chance to say yes for real, or "Can we do this tonight instead?"

**2. Say what you need.** "I don't need advice, I just need to say it" saves everyone a lot of frustration. Or, "I'd love your take after I get it out."

**3. Set a timer.** Seriously. Ten minutes of venting is usually plenty. Long enough to empty the tank, short enough to keep it from turning into a loop.

**4. Land the plane.** End with one sentence about what's next, even if it's small. "Okay. I'm going to sleep on it and decide tomorrow." This tells your body the emergency is over.

**5. Switch seats.** Ask how they're doing. Actually listen. Friendship is a two-way street, and good venting doesn't leave someone else carrying everything.

## What's the difference between venting and complaining?

Venting is releasing a feeling so you can think again. Complaining is circling the same problem without wanting anything to change.

A quick way to tell: after venting, you usually feel lighter. After complaining, you usually feel more wound up. If you've told the same story five times and it still makes your jaw clench, you may not need to vent again. You may need to do something about it, or let it go.

## How do I vent when I'm really angry?

Anger is a lot of energy in a body. Before you talk, give that energy somewhere to go:

- **Breathe out long.** In for four, out for eight, a few times. Long exhales help your body slow down.
- **Move.** Walk around the block. Shake out your hands. Climb some stairs.
- **Write it first.** Get the raw version on paper. No editing. Then you can talk about it more calmly.

## Try this: the 3-part vent

Next time you need to vent, try this structure, alone or with a friend:

1. **What happened?** Just the facts, in two or three sentences.
2. **What did I feel?** Name it. Angry, hurt, embarrassed, scared, disappointed. Often there's more than one.
3. **What do I want now?** An apology? A change? Some rest? To never think about it again?

That third question is where the magic is. It turns a vent into a next step.

## Where can I vent if my friends are tapped out?

Sometimes your friends really are maxed out. Or the thing you need to vent about involves them. Or you just don't want to tell this one to anyone in your life.

A few options:

- **A journal.** Free, private and always awake.
