"How do I do a vagus nerve reset?"

Maybe you've seen the videos. Ice on your face. Humming. Lying on your back with your hands behind your head. "Reset your vagus nerve in 60 seconds and feel amazing."

It's tempting. When you're anxious, wired or exhausted, a single switch sounds wonderful.

So let's be the honest friend here. Some of this is helpful. Some of it is oversold. Here's how to tell the difference.

## What is a vagus nerve reset?

Your vagus nerve is a real nerve that runs from your brainstem down through your neck, chest and belly. It's part of the system that helps your body slow down, rest and digest.

A "vagus nerve reset" is an internet phrase, not a medical one. It usually means a quick practice meant to help you shift from "on alert" to "calmer." Long exhales, humming, cool water, slow eye movements, gentle stretches.

Those practices are real, old and useful. The word "reset" is the part that can mislead you.

## What can a vagus nerve reset do?

Done regularly, these simple practices can:

- **Help you feel calmer in the moment.** Many people notice their breath slows and their shoulders drop.
- **Interrupt a spiral.** A minute of humming or slow breathing can give your brain a break from the loop.
- **Give you a pause before you react.** That pause is gold. It's often the difference between the text you send and the text you regret.
- **Build a habit of noticing.** The more often you check in with your body, the earlier you catch stress building.

That's genuinely valuable. Don't let anyone talk you out of it.

## What can't a vagus nerve reset do?

Here's where the hype goes too far. A vagus nerve reset can't:

- **Fix the thing that's stressing you.** If your job, your family, your money or your health is the problem, breathing helps you face it. It doesn't make it go away.
- **Cure or treat a medical or mental health condition.** If you're dealing with ongoing anxiety, depression, trauma or a physical condition, please work with your doctor or a licensed therapist. That's what they're for.
- **Replace sleep, food, daylight and people.** No 60-second trick outruns a body that's running on empty.
- **Work the same for everyone, every time.** Some days it clicks. Some days it barely touches the sides. That's normal.

If you try it and still feel awful, you haven't failed. It just means you need more than a technique right now.

## How do I try a simple vagus nerve reset?

Here's a gentle, low-stakes version. About three minutes.

1. **Sit or lie down** somewhere you won't be interrupted.
2. **Exhale first.** Let all your air out slowly, like a long sigh.
3. **Breathe in for four, out for six to eight.** Five rounds.
4. **Hum on the next three out-breaths.** One low note. Feel the buzz in your chest.
5. **Turn your head slowly** to look over one shoulder, then the other. Let your eyes lead.
6. **Notice one thing that's okay right now.** The chair under you. The warm mug. The quiet.
