"I need a life coach. One who gets what it's like to be a woman doing all of this."

All of this. The job. The kids, or the parents, or both. The calendar that lives in your head. The birthday cards, the permission slips, the "Mom, where's my..." The meeting where you said the smart thing and a coworker got credit for it ten minutes later.

You're capable. That's the problem. Capable women get handed everything.

## Why would a woman want a life coach specifically for women?

Not because women need fixing. Because a lot of what women carry is invisible, and a coach who doesn't see it can't help you put it down.

A coach who works with women understands things like:

- **The mental load.** Remembering, planning and worrying for everyone, all day, unpaid.
- **The guilt tax.** Feeling bad for resting, for spending on yourself, for saying no.
- **The people-pleasing reflex.** Saying yes before you've even checked if you want to.
- **Midlife reinvention.** Waking up at 45 or 55 and realizing you've been living someone else's plan.
- **Money and worth.** Undercharging, overgiving, never asking.

Here's what Tracee says about all of it: a woman's time, energy and labor belong to her. Not to her boss. Not to her family's expectations. Not to the version of her she was raised to be. Her.

That's not selfish. That's the starting point.

## What do women most want help with in coaching?

Usually, some mix of these:

1. **Time.** "I have no time for me." Not even ten minutes.
2. **Money.** The numbers, sure, but mostly the feelings around them.
3. **Purpose.** "Is this it?"
4. **Boundaries.** How to stop being everyone's default.
5. **Decisions.** The big one you keep circling at 2 am.
6. **Self.** "I've been who everyone needs for so long, I don't know who I am."

If that last one made your chest tighten a little, you're not alone. And you're not too late.

## What should I look for in a life coach for women?

- **She's been through it.** Lived experience counts for a lot.
- **She won't let you off the hook.** Gentle is nice. Gentle and honest is better.
- **She doesn't add to your load.** No homework so heavy it becomes one more thing you're failing at.
- **She knows her lane.** If you need a doctor or a licensed therapist, she'll say so.
- **She doesn't guarantee results.** Real change is real work.

## Try this: who owns your hours?

Take ten minutes tonight. You'll need a pen.

1. **Write down yesterday, hour by hour.** From waking up to falling asleep. Be honest.
2. **Next to each hour, write who it belonged to.** Your boss. Your kids. Your mom. The house. You.
3. **Count the hours that were yours.** Not "yours while folding laundry." Actually yours.
4. **Circle one thing to drop, delegate or do worse.** Yes, worse. Not everything needs to be perfect.
5. **Claim one hour this week.** Put it on the calendar. Treat it like a meeting you can't move.

Most women are shocked by step three. That number is information, not a judgment. It's where you start.
