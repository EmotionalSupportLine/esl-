"Do I need financial coaching? Or a financial advisor? Or do I just need to deal with how I feel about money?"

Good question, and an important one. These three get mixed up all the time, and choosing the wrong one can cost you money, time, or both.

**Let's say this clearly up front:** we are not financial advisors. We don't give financial advice, recommend investments, or tell you what to do with your money. What we do is the emotional side. This post will help you figure out which kind of help you actually need.

## The quick answer

| I need help with... | Consider |
|---|---|
| Investing, retirement, taxes, insurance, estate planning | A qualified financial advisor or planner |
| A budget, a debt payoff plan, spending habits, accountability | A financial coach |
| The feelings, fears and old stories behind my money choices | Money mindset work and emotional support |
| A money crisis like eviction or utility shutoff | Local assistance programs or a nonprofit credit counselor |

Many people need more than one. That's normal.

## What is financial advice?

Financial advice is guidance on specific money decisions: what to invest in, how to save for retirement, how to handle taxes, what insurance to buy.

People who give investment advice for pay are generally regulated. Many hold credentials, and you can look up advisors and brokers through public tools like FINRA BrokerCheck or the SEC's adviser search. Before you hire anyone, ask how they're paid (flat fee, hourly, percentage, or commission) and whether they're required to act in your best interest.

When to go here: when the decision has real numbers and real consequences. That's a job for a qualified professional.

## What is financial coaching?

Financial coaching is about behavior and habits. A financial coach helps you build a budget you'll actually use, make a plan to pay down debt, track your spending, and stick with it.

Think of it as a personal trainer for your money habits. They help with the "how" and the follow-through.

A good financial coach won't recommend specific investments or products. If they start doing that, ask whether they're licensed to.

When to go here: when you know roughly what you should do, but you keep not doing it, and you want structure and accountability.

## What is money mindset work?

Money mindset work is about the emotional layer underneath. The fear that freezes you when you open a bill. The guilt when you spend on yourself. The story you inherited about rich people, or about never having enough.

This is where we come in. It's not about the numbers. It's about why the numbers feel so heavy, and how to settle your body enough to make clear choices. (Money worry is also one of the most common reasons people end up [feeling stuck in life](/blog/feeling-stuck-in-life).)

When to go here: when you have the information, maybe even the plan, but your feelings keep hijacking it.

## Why does the emotional side matter so much?

Because you can have a perfect budget and still panic-spend at midnight. You can have a great advisor and still avoid opening their emails.

When money feels unsafe, your nervous system goes into fight-or-flight. And in fight-or-flight, nobody makes good money decisions. Settling first isn't soft. It's practical.

## Try this: which kind of help do I need?

Answer yes or no:

1. Do I have a specific decision with big numbers attached (investing, retirement, a large purchase, taxes)?
2. Do I know what I should do, but keep not doing it?
3. Do I feel sick, frozen or ashamed when I think about money?
4. Is there an urgent money emergency this month?

**Yes to 1:** start with a qualified financial professional.
**Yes to 2:** a financial coach could be a good fit.
**Yes to 3:** start with money mindset and emotional support.
**Yes to 4:** reach out to local assistance or a nonprofit credit counselor first.

More than one yes? Start with whichever feels most urgent, and know the others can come next.
