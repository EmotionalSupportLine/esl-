Nervous system regulation in real life: a reset that doesn't have to be a solo project

What nervous system regulation actually means, what a dysregulated nervous system can feel like, what a "vagus nerve reset" can and can't do, and a free 10-minute reset you can use today.

"Regulate your nervous system" is everywhere right now. So is "vagus nerve reset." Underneath the trend is something real and useful, and simpler than the internet makes it look.

What is nervous system regulation?

Your nervous system is constantly shifting between states: alert and ready to act, calm and connected, and sometimes shut down and numb. Regulation isn't about being calm all the time. It's about being able to come back to steady after something stirs you up.

A regulated day still has stress in it. You just don't stay stuck in it.

What can a dysregulated nervous system feel like?

Everyone's different, but common signs include:

  • Feeling wired and exhausted at the same time
  • Snapping at people, then feeling guilty
  • Racing thoughts, especially at night
  • Going numb, scrolling for hours, or not being able to start anything
  • Feeling like everything is urgent

These are common human responses to too much for too long, not a diagnosis. If symptoms are severe or not going away, talk to your doctor or a licensed professional.

What a "vagus nerve reset" can and can't do

The vagus nerve is part of how your body shifts into rest. Slow breathing with long exhales, humming, singing and gentle movement are simple ways many people find settling, and they're free.

What they can't do is fix the reason you keep getting overwhelmed. If your life has too much in it, or you're carrying something alone, a two-minute hack will help for two minutes. That's still worth something. It's just not the whole answer.

A nervous system reset that includes people

Most resets stop at breathing. Ours has one more step, because humans regulate with humans. The free 10-Minute Human Reset walks you through:

  1. Name it. Three words for what's going on inside.
  2. Breathe out longer than you breathe in. Ten slow rounds.
  3. Look around. Five things you can see, three you can hear.
  4. Feel your feet and your weight. Ground on the floor, or on the earth.
  5. Reach for a human. Text, call or talk to someone steady.

If you've done every breathing exercise and still feel flooded, that's not failure. It might mean you need co-regulation: a calm person to talk it through with. Book a 30-minute call, or start with the 2-minute quiz to see what you need right now.
