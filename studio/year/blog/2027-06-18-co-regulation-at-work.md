"Why does the whole team fall apart when one person panics?"

You've seen it. One stressed message lands in the group chat, and within an hour everyone's typing faster, replying shorter and quietly updating their résumés.

And you've seen the opposite too. One steady person walks into a tense meeting, and somehow the temperature drops.

That's co-regulation at work. And it might be the most underrated skill in your office.

## What is co-regulation at work?

Co-regulation is when one person's calm helps another person's nervous system settle. It happens naturally between people all the time, at home, with friends and, yes, at work.

When a colleague is grounded, the people around them tend to settle. When a manager is frantic, the team tends to speed up and tense up. Stress spreads. So does steadiness.

(Here's our plain-English primer: [what is co-regulation?](/blog/what-is-co-regulation))

## Why does co-regulation matter for teams?

Because stressed people don't do their best thinking. When your body is in fight-or-flight, your focus narrows. You react instead of respond. You miss things. You snap at people you like.

A team that can settle together tends to:

- Make clearer decisions under pressure.
- Recover faster after a hard meeting or a mistake.
- Speak up about problems sooner, instead of hiding them.
- Trust each other more.

You don't need a big program for this. You need a few people willing to be the calm in the room.

## What does a calm colleague actually do?

It's mostly small, quiet stuff:

- **They slow down.** They speak a little slower and lower when things heat up.
- **They name it.** "This is a lot. Let's take a breath before we decide."
- **They stay present.** No scrolling during the hard conversation.
- **They don't add fuel.** No gossip, no catastrophizing, no "we're all doomed."
- **They ask, then listen.** "What do you need right now?" and then actually wait.

Anyone can do this. Not just managers. A calm intern can steady a room too.

## How can managers co-regulate their teams?

If you lead people, your nervous system sets the tone, whether you like it or not. A few ideas:

- **Regulate yourself before the meeting.** Two minutes of long exhales in your car or at your desk.
- **Open hard meetings with a pause.** "Before we dive in, let's all take one slow breath."
- **Lower the urgency where you can.** Not everything is a fire. Say so.
- **Model taking breaks.** If you never step away, your team won't either.
- **Check in one on one.** A five-minute "How are you, really?" goes a long way.

## How do I practice co-regulation in a stressful meeting?

Here's a simple practice we call **The Steady Three**. Nobody even has to know you're doing it.

1. **Feel your feet.** Press them into the floor under the table. Notice the chair holding you up.
2. **Lengthen your exhale.** Breathe in for four, out for six. Quietly. Three to five rounds.
3. **Soften your voice.** When you speak next, go a little slower and a little lower than everyone else.

Watch what happens. Often, people match you without realizing it.
