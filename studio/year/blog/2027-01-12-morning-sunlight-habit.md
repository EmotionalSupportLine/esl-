There's a habit that costs nothing, takes a few minutes and needs no equipment: stepping outside into morning light.

It's become popular for good reason. Many people find that getting outside early in the day helps them feel more awake in the morning and more ready for sleep at night. Our bodies keep time partly by light, and a lot of us spend our days indoors under the same dim, flat lighting from morning to night.

We're not going to promise it will change your life. But it's one of the easiest small habits to try, and winter is when many of us need it most.

## What a morning light habit looks like

The idea is simple: within the first hour or two of waking, go outside and let natural daylight reach your eyes.

- **Go outside.** Light through a window is much dimmer than light outdoors, even on a cloudy day.
- **Don't look at the sun.** Just be outside, facing the general direction of the sky.
- **Skip the sunglasses** for these few minutes if it's comfortable and safe for you.
- **Start with 5 to 10 minutes.** On very overcast days, a little longer.

If you have an eye condition or a medical reason to limit sun exposure, check with your doctor first.

## Making it work in winter

Winter mornings can be dark, cold and rushed. A few ideas:

**Pair it with something you already do.** Drink your coffee on the porch. Walk the dog. Take a phone call outside.

**Go when the light arrives.** In January, sunrise may be after you've started work. Step out during your first break instead.

**Dress for it.** Keep a coat, hat and slippers by the door so there's no excuse.

**Count cloudy days too.** Even gray daylight outdoors is far brighter than indoor lighting.

## Why it feels good beyond the light

Like many small outdoor habits, the benefits aren't only about light:

- A few quiet minutes before the day's demands begin
- Fresh air and a change of scene
- Noticing the sky, the birds and the season
- A sense of having done one kind thing for yourself, first thing

Tomorrow morning, before you check your messages, try stepping outside. Look up. Breathe. That's the whole habit.
