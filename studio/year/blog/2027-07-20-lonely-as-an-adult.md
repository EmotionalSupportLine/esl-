Making friends used to happen almost by accident. You sat next to someone in class. You were on the same team. You lived down the hall.

As adults, it doesn't work that way anymore. People move, have kids, change jobs, get busy. Before you know it, you realize you don't really have anyone to call.

If that's you, you're far from alone, and it's not a sign that something is wrong with you.

## Why adult friendship is harder

- **Fewer natural meeting places.** No school, no dorm, fewer clubs.
- **Busier lives.** Work and family eat most of our time.
- **More hesitation.** We worry about seeming needy or weird.
- **Life transitions.** Moves, divorce, retirement and kids leaving home can all thin out a social circle.

## Small ways to start reconnecting

**Reach out to one old friend.** A simple text: "I was thinking about you. How are you?" Many people are just as lonely and just as hesitant to make the first move.

**Show up regularly somewhere.** Friendships tend to grow from repeated contact: the same class, walking group, volunteer shift, or coffee shop. Choose one and keep going.

**Join something with a shared interest.** Gardening club, hiking group, choir, a book club, a craft class. Shared activities take the pressure off conversation.

**Say yes to invitations, even small ones.** And extend them too. "Want to grab a coffee after this?"

**Be patient.** Close friendships take time. Many small, repeated moments of connection add up.

**Value smaller connections too.** A friendly neighbor, a chat with the barista. Light connections matter more than we think.

## When loneliness feels heavy

If loneliness is affecting your mood, sleep or sense of hope, it's worth talking to someone about it. A support call gives you 30 minutes with a kind, trained person. And if loneliness ever leads to thoughts of suicide, please call or text 988 (US).

Connection usually starts small. One text, one class, one call.
