Some decisions are easy. Others sit in your chest for weeks. Stay or go. Say yes or say no. This job or that one. Move or stay put.

When every option feels a little wrong, it's tempting to keep thinking until the "right" answer appears. Usually it doesn't. Here's a gentler way through.

## Why hard decisions feel so hard

Most hard decisions have three things in common:

- **There's no perfect option.** Every path has a cost.
- **Other people have opinions.** Their voices get mixed in with yours.
- **You're scared of regret.** So you wait, hoping certainty will arrive.

Waiting is a decision too, and it often costs more than either choice.

## A five-step way through

### 1. Write the actual decision in one sentence

"Should I change my life?" is too big. "Should I accept the job offer in Denver by February 15?" is a decision you can make.

### 2. Separate the voices

On paper, list what *you* want, what you think you *should* want and what other people want for you. Seeing them apart is often a relief. You might notice the loudest voice isn't yours.

### 3. Imagine living each option for a month

Picture yourself a month from now, having chosen option A. Walk through an ordinary Tuesday. How does your body feel? Lighter, heavier, tighter, relieved? Then do the same for option B. Your body often knows before your mind does.

### 4. Ask what you'd regret not trying

Regret tends to sting more for the things we never tried than for the things that didn't work out. Which choice would you wonder about in ten years?

### 5. Choose a "good enough" answer and a check-in date

You don't need certainty. You need a reasonable choice and a plan to review it: "I'll try this for three months and then reassess." Decisions feel lighter when they aren't forever.

## Talk it through out loud

Many people find a decision becomes clearer the moment they say it out loud to someone neutral. A support call gives you 30 minutes with a trained supporter who won't push you either way. We'll help you hear your own answer, then name one next step.

You don't have to be sure. You just have to take the next step.
