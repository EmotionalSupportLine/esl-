Listen to the voice in your head on a hard day. What does it say?

For a lot of us, it sounds something like: *You should be further along. Everyone else is managing. What's wrong with you?*

We would never talk to a friend like that. But we talk to ourselves like that every day. Here's how to soften that voice, a little at a time.

## Why we're so hard on ourselves

Many of us learned early that being tough on ourselves was how we stayed safe, got things done or earned approval. The critical voice thinks it's helping. It's trying to protect you from failure.

But constant criticism rarely makes us do better. More often, it makes us freeze, hide or give up. Kindness isn't the same as letting yourself off the hook. It's the steady ground that lets you try again.

## Five ways to be kinder to yourself

### 1. Use the friend test

When you catch yourself being harsh, ask: *what would I say to a friend in this exact situation?* Then say that to yourself, out loud if you can.

### 2. Name the feeling, not the flaw

"I'm a failure" is a flaw. "I feel disappointed and tired" is a feeling. Feelings pass. Naming them accurately makes them easier to hold.

### 3. Put a hand on your heart

It sounds simple, but many people find that a hand on the chest and a slow breath brings a sense of comfort. Your body responds to kindness, even from you.

### 4. Remember you're not the only one

Struggle isn't proof that you're uniquely broken. It's proof that you're human. Everyone you admire has had days exactly like this one.

### 5. Do one small caring thing

Make a warm drink. Step outside. Take a shower. Go to bed early. Kindness is often an action, not a thought.

## On the really hard days

Some days, all you can do is get through. That counts. You don't have to feel grateful, productive or positive. You can simply be tired and still be worthy of care.
