The ground is warming up. The grass is turning green. If you've been curious about earthing (spending time barefoot in direct contact with the earth), spring is the easiest time to start.

Here's a simple beginner's plan.

## Quick refresher: what earthing is

Earthing, also called grounding, means letting your bare skin touch natural ground: grass, soil, sand or stone. Research on the topic is still early, so we won't make big claims. What many people report is simpler: it feels calming, it gets them outside and it pulls their attention back into their body.

## A two-week beginner plan

**Days 1 to 3: two minutes.** Step outside barefoot onto grass or soil. Stand still. Notice the temperature and texture under your feet. Breathe.

**Days 4 to 7: five minutes.** Walk slowly around the yard or a park lawn. Or sit with your feet on the grass while you drink your coffee.

**Days 8 to 14: ten minutes or more.** Pair it with something you already do: a phone call, reading, watching the kids or the dog play. Try different surfaces: damp grass, garden soil, sand, a smooth rock.

That's it. No special gear, no rules about the perfect time of day.

## Where to do it

- Your own lawn or garden
- A local park (check the grass for anything sharp first)
- The beach or a lakeshore
- A trail with soft dirt, walked slowly

Concrete, asphalt and wooden decks don't count in the traditional sense, but they're still better than staying indoors.

## Safety first

- Check the ground for glass, thorns, sharp sticks and animal mess
- Avoid lawns that were recently sprayed with chemicals
- Skip it if the ground is icy or very hot
- If you have diabetes, poor circulation or any foot problems, ask your doctor before going barefoot outside
- Wash your feet afterward

## Make it stick

**Lower the bar.** Two minutes counts. Small and consistent beats long and occasional.

**Make it visible.** Leave a pair of slip-on shoes by the back door so you can kick them off easily.

**Track it.** Seeing a streak build is surprisingly motivating.
