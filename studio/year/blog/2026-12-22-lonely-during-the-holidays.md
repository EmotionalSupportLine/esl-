Everywhere you look this week, there are pictures of full tables and crowded living rooms. If your life doesn't look like that right now, the contrast can sting.

Maybe you're spending the holidays alone. Maybe you're surrounded by people and still feel unseen. Maybe someone is missing this year. Loneliness during the holidays is far more common than the ads would have you believe, and it doesn't mean anything is wrong with you.

## Why the holidays make loneliness louder

The season carries a lot of expectations. Family, togetherness, joy. When reality doesn't match, it can feel like everyone else got an invitation you didn't.

There's also more quiet time. Work slows down, routines disappear, and the feelings we usually keep busy enough to ignore come to the surface.

## If you're spending the holidays alone

**Make a loose plan for the hard days.** Unstructured time is where loneliness grows. Plan a few small anchors: a morning walk, a favorite meal, a movie, a call with someone at a set time.

**Get outside at least once a day.** A walk around the neighborhood, a park bench, the sky. Being out among the world, even quietly, helps you feel part of it.

**Choose your social media carefully.** If scrolling makes it worse, take a break for a few days. Highlight reels aren't the whole story.

**Do one small thing for someone else.** Text an old friend, leave a kind note, volunteer for an afternoon. Connection often begins with giving a little.

**Create a tradition that's just yours.** A long bath on Christmas Eve, a special breakfast, a solo hike, a book you save for the day. Traditions don't need a crowd.

## If you're lonely in a full room

Sometimes the loneliest place is a table full of people who don't really know you. If that's you:

- Find one person you can be a little more honest with, even briefly
- Step outside for a breather when you need one
- Remind yourself that feeling unseen today doesn't mean you're unseeable

## If you're missing someone

Grief often gets louder at the holidays. It's okay to be sad. You can set a place for them, tell a story about them, or step away when it's too much. There's no right way to do this.
