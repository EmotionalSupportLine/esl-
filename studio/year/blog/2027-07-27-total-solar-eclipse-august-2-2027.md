On **Monday, August 2, 2027**, the moon passes directly in front of the sun and turns day into night along a narrow path across the Earth. It's a total solar eclipse, and it's one of the longest of the century.

## Where to see it

The path of totality crosses southern Spain, North Africa (including Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya and Egypt) and parts of the Middle East. Near the Egyptian city of Luxor, totality lasts more than six minutes, which is extraordinarily long.

From the United States, this eclipse isn't visible. If you're traveling to see it, plan well ahead, and always use certified solar eclipse glasses for any part of the eclipse that isn't totality. Never look directly at the sun without proper protection.

## What a total solar eclipse is

A solar eclipse happens at a new moon, when the moon lines up exactly between the Earth and the sun. During totality, the moon covers the sun completely. The sky darkens, the temperature drops and the sun's glowing outer atmosphere (the corona) becomes visible.

People who've seen totality often describe it as one of the most moving experiences of their lives.

## Eclipse season, in astrology

This eclipse falls at a **new moon in Leo**, in an eclipse season that also includes penumbral lunar eclipses on July 18 and August 17.

In astrology, solar eclipses are often described as powerful new beginnings, like a new moon with the volume turned up. Leo is associated with creativity, self-expression, joy, courage and the heart.
